“Section 164. An Assessor or Assistant Commissioner shall give

notice of assessment to each person and each partnership who

or which has been assessed, stating the amount of income

assessed and the amount of tax charged….”

Accordingly, the letter of intimation under Section 163 is not an

assessment. Merely issuing assessment is not considered as a full

assessment. It has to be notified to the Tax Payer. It is the common

sense that once you notice, the Assessment get the full status of

Assessment. One making an assessment and keeping it in the

drawyer, will not be an Assessment. Only once it is notified the Tax

Payer, the same would get the full status of an Assessment.

In Fonterra Brands Lanka (PVT) Ltd Vs CGIR, et al, SC Appeal

187/2014, it was held that the assessment is completed only when

the notice of Assessment is received. His Lordship Justice S

Thurairajah held:

“I hold that an “assessment” is not complete until it is

communicated to the taxpayer by notice of assessment served

within the time limit. The statutory bar applies to the

assessment as a whole, including notice. A letter of intimation is

not an assessment, nor is it an internal department calculation.”

Judgment CA Tax No. 18/2021 Page 16|18

In Commissioner General of Inland Revenue Vs Commercial

Leasing and Finance Ltd, CA/TAX/13/2021. This bench has held:

“Accordingly, it must be emphasized that the assessment

process is completed only upon communication of the

assessment to the specific taxpayer. A mere internal

computation or documentation by the Assessor is insufficient

and only the delivery of the formal notice fulfils legal

requirement of the assessment under the VAT Act.

Furthermore, considering the nature of this statute without the

notice of assessment being provided to the Tax Payer, the Tax

Payer will be precluded from exercising their statutory rights

thereafter. Moreover, restricting the time limits under Section 33

(1) of the VAT Act solely to the “making” of the assessment

would grant the Appellant an unreasonable advantage,

enabling them to issue the Notice of Assessment at any time of

their choosing. In my view, this contravenes the true purpose of

the statutory time limit under Section 33(1) which must govern

both the making of the Assessment and issuance of notice to the

Tax Payer.”

Judgment CA Tax No. 18/2021 Page 17|18

There are no materials shown to Court for us to deviate from the

above judgment. By considering the above materials, this court

conclude that the Assessment has been communicated on

05.12.2014, which is well beyond the last date of which the

assessment could have been made in terms of Section 163(1) and

163(5) and hence the assessment is time barred and invalid.
