Judgement

Introduction

The Petitioners challenge two quit notices issued to them by the 1st Respondent on basis that

the land in suit is State land. The Petitioners so challenge the said two quit notices on the basis

that the land in suit is private land.

After issuance of formal notice, the Respondents have filed their Objections, and thereafter,

this was argued before me on 07.05.2026; hence, this judgement.

Factual matrix

The 1st Petitioner claims that she is the owner of the land in suit, which is in extent of Forty-

Three Acres One Rood and Ten Perches, as depicted in the plan marked as P1. The said land

is an amalgamated land of several parcels of land so depicted in the plans marked as P2 and

P3.

The 1st Petitioner asserts that she purchased this property from the predecessor in title under

and by virtue of the Deed marked as P10, attested in 2008. In addition to that, she has annexed

certain title deeds marked as P5 to P9, to establish that her predecessors in title obtained such

title based on those deeds. Furthermore, she claims that after she had become the owner, she

has paid acreage taxes, which were accepted by the relevant Pradeshiya Sabha; she also

annexes the Street Line and non-vesting Certificate to the Petition, marked as P10(b).

Her predecessors in title had obtained permission from the Provincial Fisheries Ministry and

various other authorities to carry on the business of a prawn culture project in the land in suit,

on several occasions; to establish the same, she has annexed such documents marked as P11 to

P21. Thus, the 1st Petitioner claims that this is a freeholding land, and accordingly, the issuance

4

of a quit notice in respect of the said land was instigated by the then-Member of Parliament of

the area, whose photograph is marked as P29(a) annexed to the Petition to indicate that he and

some of his supporters had gathered on the said land on the basis that it belongs to the State,

when it is in fact a private land. Therefore, the Petitioners have moved the authorities to take

legal actions against the said people, which resulted in a 66 Application filed in the Magistrate's

Court of Puttalam.

Accordingly, the Petitioners seek inter alia the following relief by way of this Application;

(c) Grant and issue an order in the nature of Writ of Certiorari quashing the Quit Notice

marked P34;

(d) Grant and issue an order in the nature of Writ of Certiorari quashing the Quit

Notice marked P35;

(e) Grant and issue an order in the nature of Writ of Certiorari quashing the

proceedings, including the orders, in the Magistrate's Court Case, if any, instituted

under State Lands (Recovery of Possession) Act No. 07 of 1979 (as amended) in

Magistrate's Court pursuant to the Quit Notice marked P34;

(f) Grant and issue an order in the nature of Writ of Certiorari quashing the

proceedings including the orders, in the Magistrate's Court Case, if any, instituted

under State Lands (Recovery of Possession) Act No. 07 of 1979 (as amended) in

Magistrate's Court pursuant to the Quit Notice marked P35;

(g) Grant and issue an order in the nature of Writ of Certiorari quashing any

order/decision/declaration (if any) made under the Land Settlement Ordinance or the

Land Acquisition Act or Title Registration Act any other Statute, vesting the 1st

Petitioner's land in the State;

5

(h) Grant and issue an order in the nature of Writ of Prohibition prohibiting the 1st to

4th Respondents and/or anyone or more of them and/or added or substitute Respondents,

from taking steps under State Lands (Recovery of Possession) Act No. 07 of 1979 (as

amended) in respect of Lot 'X' in Plan No. 1051A (marked P1) dated 10.07.2008;

By way of Objections, the 1st to 4th Respondents have moved for the dismissal of this

Application. It is their position that the land in question had been identified as State land, and

accordingly, at the request of the Divisional Secretary, the said land had been identified as State

land by the Survey General; the relevant plans are marked as 1R3(a) and 1R7. The requests

made by some authorities are marked as 1R2 and 1R6, to establish that the land in suit is State

land.

Title to the land in dispute

The Petitioners claim that the land in dispute, which is in extent of 43 Acres 01 Rood and 10

Perches, as depicted in the Plan P1 annexed to the Petition, is owned by the 1st Petitioner (a

dual citizen) whilst the 2nd Petitioner is her husband (a British citizen). They both possess the

land in suit through the 3rd Petitioner, who is the caretaker of the said property.

Though the Petitioners claim that the 1st Petitioner owns the land in suit by virtue of the Deeds

marked as P5 to P9, she appears to have purchased the property by virtue of Deed P10 and

dated 23.07.2008. According to the Schedule of P10, the 1st Petitioner has obtained her title for

a land in extent of 43 Acres 01 Rood and 10 Perches, which is the land depicted in the Plan

marked as P1.

However, though the 1st Petitioner claims that the said land is an amalgamation of several

parcels of land, on a perusal of the other deeds marked as P5 to P9, no connection can be seen

and how the parcels of land have been amalgamated cannot be seen. Nevertheless, the 1st

Petitioner claims that the Deeds marked as P5 to P9 are the title deeds of her predecessors in

6

title. However, it appears that there is no chain of title commencing from one particular point

of time, or how these lands have been amalgamated, except for the Deed marked as P10, by

which the 1st Petitioner became the owner of the said 43 Acres 01 Rood and 10 Perches in 2008.

If at all, it should be presumed that the land came into existence in 2008 as depicted in P1,

which is dated 10.07.2008. Prior to this, the land in suit must have been, if at all, existed as

several lands, which is also borne out by the fact what is borne out by the documents marked

as P2 and P3 are in relation to different lands which later appear to have been amalgamated by

P1; the documents marked as P2 and P3 are dated 01.07.2008, which means that within the

span of about nine days, the lands have been amalgamated for the land depicted in P1 to come

into existence. A few days thereafter only has the Deed marked as P10 has been executed,

dated 23.07.2008.

Therefore, it appears that the 1st Petitioner's title should commence from those title deeds and

title plans, and beyond that, the 1st Petitioner has not placed before this Court what sort of title

she has got. In fact, on a perusal of the other deeds marked and submitted to this Court as P5

to P9, it appears that different people had claimed title (who, as the Petitioners claim were the

predecessors in title); however, their title had never gone beyond those title deeds, while it

appears that some of them have also claimed title by way of deed of declarations as well.

Therefore, in any case, this Court cannot go into the matters relating to title as such; if at all, if

the Petitioners have title and have acquired the paper title or prescriptive title, even against the

State, they should have had a properly formulated and constituted case filed before the relevant

forum and have themselves declared as the true owners against anybody, including the

Respondents.

7

In support of my view, I rely upon the dictum of His Lordship Justice Ranasinghe in the case

of Thajudeen v. Sri Lanka Tea Board and Another,1 where it was held as thus;

All such disputed matters of fact must be resolved before a mandatory order, such as is

claimed by the Petitioner in these proceedings, goes out from this Court. The issuance

of such an order carries with it the implication that this Court is satisfied that the said

amount is in fact due to the Petitioner and that there is no question about the basic

primary questions of fact upon which the Petitioner's claim is founded When, however,

such questions of fact are in dispute they can and must only be settled by a regular

action between the disputants before the appropriate Court of First Instance. Such

questions, the decision of which calls for the leading of evidence, both oral and

documentary and the cross-examination of witnesses are all questions which can be

best decided by way of regular procedure falling within the ordinary jurisdiction of the

Courts of First Instance (at page 474).

Dispute arose in 2015

The second matter that should be taken into consideration is that as borne out by the document

marked as 1R1 to 1R3(b), there had been certain disputes with regard to the clearance of the

jungle area and cutting down of trees in the area close to the Uslantivu Forest reserve. As such,

in August 2015 by 1R1, the Grama Niladhari of the area has reported the matter, and the person

responsible for cutting down the said trees is also mentioned in 1R2 (dated 20.11.2015), as

A.M. Nawas, who is supposed to be one of the predecessors in title of the 1st Petitioner.

Thereafter, the 1st Respondent had requested the Surveyor Superintendent of Puttalam to survey

the land to identify the State lands as the said Nawas had felled the trees standing on State land;

the said survey plan is marked as 1R3(a) annexed to the Objections.

1 [1981] 2 Sri L.R. 471.

8

These documents marked as 1R1 to 1R3(b) clearly indicate that the dispute, if any, on whether

the land is claimed by the Petitioners or is supposed to be State land, arose in 2015. Thus, the

Petitioners should have immediately gone to the relevant District Court and attempted to

resolve the matter, which they have failed to do. They waited until the 1st Respondent had

obtained the Government Survey's Plan marked as 1R7 and formed his opinion in terms of

Section 3 of the State Lands (Recovery of Possession) Act, No. 07 of 1979. Once the 1st

Respondent had formed his opinion, it is his duty under the said Act to recover the same from

an unlawful possession, and accordingly, he has taken the correct steps as it appears in this

case.

Therefore, if at all, when the dispute arose between the parties in 2015, the Petitioner should

have gone before the District Court and have their land declared as belonging to them, which

they have failed to do. Though the Petitioners claim that they are possessing the land through

the 3rd Petitioner, who is the caretaker, although they may be far away (one in Colombo and

the other in England), they have constructive possession of the land in suit. In such a scenario,

it is presumed that they are aware of what is happening in situ. As such, they cannot deny the

fact that behind their backs, without notice to them, the Respondents had surreptitiously

surveyed and prepared the plan 1R7. As such, it is my view that if they had any claim, they

should have gone to the District Court.

Accordingly, it is my view that no writ lies based on the documents placed before this Court

by the Petitioners themselves.

Conclusion

As such, I dismiss this Application, without costs.

JUDGE OF THE COURT OF APPEAL
