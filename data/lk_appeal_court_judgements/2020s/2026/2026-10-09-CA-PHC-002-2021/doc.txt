K. M. S. DISSANAYAKE, J.

Instant appeal arises from an order dated 27.01.2021, made by the learned

High Court Judge of the North Western Province holden in Kurunegala

(hereinafter called and referred to as “the learned High Court Judge”) in the

exercise of the revisionary jurisdiction vested in him under and in terms of

Article 154P(3)(b) of the Constitution of the Democratic Socialist Republic of

Sri Lanka (hereinafter called and referred to as “the Constitution”) wherein,

CA(PHC) 0002/21

Page 5 of 10

the learned High Court Judge had dismissed an application in revision

preferred to the High Court of the North Western Province holden in

Kurunegala (hereinafter called and referred to as “the High Court”) from an

order made by the learned Magistrate of Galgamuwa dated 07.06.2017

(hereinafter called and referred to as “the MC order”) in respect of a certificate

issued to it by the Employees’ Trust Fund Board who being the Plaintiff-

Respondent-Respondent to the instant appeal (hereinafter called and referred

to as “the Plaintiff-Respondent”) under and in terms of section 28(3) of the

Employees’ Trust Fund Act No. 46 of 1980 (hereinafter called and referred to

as “the Act”) in an action bearing No. 7212/Employees Trust Fund.

The Petitioner-Appellant (hereinafter called and referred to as the “Appellant”)

seeks to impugn the order of the learned High Court Judge dated 27.01.2021

(hereinafter called and referred to as “the HC order”) on the grounds of appeal

as morefully, urged by him in paragraph 6(අ) to (උ) of the petition of appeal.

Upon a careful analysis of the grounds of appeal so urged by the Appellant, it

clearly, appears that the instant Appeal is mainly, three-fold, namely;

a) The learned High Court Judge had failed to appreciate that, the

Appellant had been made a Defendant to the said action filed by the

Plaintiff-Respondent in the Magistrate Court of Galgamuwa by reason

of the fact that, his name had merely, been stated in the relevant

documents as a Director of Echo Uniware (Pvt) Ltd;

b) The learned High Court Judge had failed to appreciate that, the learned

Magistrate of Galgamuwa had not afforded a reasonable opportunity to

the Appellant to adduce evidence before it in order to establish his

defence raised by him under the proviso to section 40 of the Act, to the

certificate issued to the Magistrate Court of Galgamuwa by the Plaintiff-

Respondent under section 28(3) thereof, thus, depriving him of his right

to a fair trial;

c) The learned High Court Judge as well as the learned Magistrate of

Galgamuwa had failed to appreciate that, miscarriage of justice had

caused to the Appellant by the Plaintiff Appellant by directly, filing a

Page 6 of 10

certificate under section 28(3) of the Act without first resorting to a Civil

Suit as provided for by the Act.

Let me now, examine the facts and the circumstances relevant to the instant

appeal.

The Chairman/Chief Executive Officer of the Employees Trust Fund Board

had issued a certificate under and in terms of section 28(3) of the Act against

the 1st Defendant-Respondent-Respondent (hereinafter called and referred to

as “the 1st Respondent”) and the Appellant being directors of the aforesaid

company- Echo Uniware (Pvt) Ltd in the Magistrate Court of Galgamuwa for

the recovery of a sum of Rs.858,281.57/- as contributions and surcharge

against them; and that the learned Magistrate of Galgamuwa had summoned

the Appellant before him to show cause why further proceedings the recovery

of the sum due under the Act should not be taken against him; and that the

Appellant had shown cause in writing as to why further proceedings for the

recovery of the sum due under the Act should not be taken against him; and

that the learned Magistrate of Galgamuwa had in the MC order, having

rejected the cause shown by the Appellant, proceeded to recover the sum

mentioned in the certificate from the Appellant as a fine as provided for by

section 291 of the Code of Criminal Procedure Act No. 15 of 1979; and that

being aggrieved thereby, the Appellant had preferred to the High Court an

application in revision seeking to revise and set aside the order of the learned

Magistrate of Galgamuwa (MC order); and that the learned High Court Judge

had in the HC order, having inquired into the application in revision, rejected

it; and that being aggrieved thereby, instant appeal has now, been preferred

to this Court by the Appellant impugning the HC order and the MC order as

well.

It is in this context, let me now, examine section 28(3) of the Act,

“(3) Where an employer makes default in the payment of any sum which he is

liable to pay under this Act and the Board is of opinion that it is impracticable

or inexpedient to recover that sum under subsection (1) or subsection (2) or

where the total amount due has not been recovered by seizure and sale, then

Page 7 of 10

the Board may issue a certificate containing particulars of the sum so due

and the name and place of residence of the defaulting employer to the

Magistrate having jurisdiction in the division in which the place of work of the

employee in respect of whom the default is made, is situate. The Magistrate

shall thereupon summon such employer before him to show cause why

further proceedings for the recovery of the sum due under this Act should not

be taken against him and in default of sufficient cause being shown, such

sum shall be deemed to be a fine imposed by a sentence of the Magistrate on

such employer for an offence punishable with imprisonment and the

provisions of section 291 (except paragraphs (a), (f) and (i) of subsection (1)

thereof) of the Code of Criminal Procedure Act relating to the default of

payment of a fine imposed for such an offence shall thereupon apply and the

Magistrate may make any directions which by the provisions of that section

he could have made at the time of imposing such a sentence.

(4) The correctness of any statement in a certificate issued by the Board for

the purpose of this section shall not be called in question or examined by the

court in any proceedings under this section and accordingly nothing in this

section shall authorize court to consider or decide the correctness of any

statement in such certificate and the certificate of the Board shall be sufficient

evidence that the amount due under this Act from the defaulting employer

has been duly calculated and that such amount is in default.”

The plain wording of section 28(4) of the Act does not admit of any doubt

and/or argument that the sum stated in the certificate issued under section

28(3) of the Act to be due, has to be accepted as correct by the learned

Magistrate.

However, the only permissible defences to recovery proceedings initiated

under section 28(3) of the Act would be;

a) The Respondent has paid the amount due;

b) The Respondent is not the defaulter;

c) The certificate has been filed in a Court which has no jurisdiction to

initiate recovery proceedings.

It is significant to observe that it had never been the defence raised by the

Appellant to the recovery proceedings initiated by the Plaintiff-Respondent

against him in the Magistrate Court of Galgamuwa under section 28(3) of the

Act.

However, the defence raised by the Appellant to the recovery proceedings

initiated by the Plaintiff-Respondent against him in the Magistrate Court of

Galgamuwa under section 28(3) of the Act was based on the premise that, the

learned High Court Judge had failed to appreciate that, the learned Magistrate

of Galgamuwa had not afforded a reasonable opportunity to the Appellant to

adduce evidence before it in order to establish his defence raised by him under

the proviso to section 40 of the Act, to the certificate issued to the Magistrate

Court of Galgamuwa by the Plaintiff-Respondent under section 28(3) thereof,

thus, depriving him of his right to a fair trial.

It is in this context, let me now, examine the section 40 of the Act and the

proviso thereto.

Section 40 of the Act reads thus;

“Where any offence under this Act is committed by a body of persons,

then

(a) if that body of persons is a body corporate, every director and officer

of that body corporate; or

(b) if that body of persons is a firm, every partner of that firm ; or

(c) if that body of persons is a trade union, every officer of that trade

union; or

(d) if that body of persons is an unincorporated body, the President,

Manager, Secretary and every other officer of that unincorporated body,

shall be deemed to be guilty of such offence:”

Page 9 of 10

The proviso to section 40 of the Act reads thus;

“Provided that no such person shall be deemed to be guilty of such offence if

he proves that such offence was committed without his knowledge or that he

exercised due diligence to prevent the commission of such offence.”

Now, the pertinent question is whether the matters stated in the proviso to

section 40 of the Act can be used by a Respondent being a defaulter under

section 28(3) of the Act, as a defence to the recovery proceedings initiated by

the Plaintiff-Respondent against him in the Magistrate Court of Galgamuwa

under section 28(3) of the Act.

It may now, be examined.

A careful analysis of the section 40 of the Act and its proviso makes it manifest

that, the defence set out in the proviso to section 40 of the Act can only, be

available only in case where a body of persons is charged with for having

committed an offence under the Act. However, the proceedings initiated by

the Petitioner-Respondent against the Appellant in the instant appeal under

section 28(3) of the Act, are recovery proceedings simpliciter and not the

criminal proceedings initiated under section 40 of the Act and therefore, the

proceedings pertaining to the instant action does not in any manner, come

within the ambit of section 40 of the Act and therefore, a defence set out in

proviso to section 40 of the Act is not in any manner, available to the Appellant

in the instant appeal in the proceedings which are initiated by the Petitioner-

Respondent against the Appellant in the instant appeal under section 28(3) of

the Act.

I would therefore hold, that the ground so urged by the Appellant in the

instant appeal in support thereof, is totally, misconceived in law and as such

it is not entitled to succeed both in fact and law and therefore, it ought to be

rejected in-limine.

The next ground urged by the Appellant in support of the appeal being that

the learned High Court Judge as well as the learned Magistrate of Galgamuwa

had failed to appreciate that, miscarriage of justice had caused to the

CA(PHC) 0002/21

Page 10 of 10

Appellant by the Plaintiff Appellant by directly, filing a certificate under

section 28(3) of the Act without first resorting to a Civil Suit as provided for

by the Act.

It may now, be examined.

It is now, well settled law that, the Commissioner of Labour has a discretion

in deciding between the procedure set out in section 28(1), 28(2) and 28(3) of

the Act. This view taken by me is well supported and fortified by the decision

of this Court in CA(PHC) 140/2014-CAM-28.06.2018.

I would therefore, hold that the next contention so advanced by the Appellant

in support of the appeal too, is devoid of merit and therefore, it too, should be

rejected in limine.

In view of all the above circumstances, I would hold that, the appeal is not

entitled to succeed both in fact and law.

In the result, I would proceed to dismiss the appeal with costs fixed at Rs.

75,000/- payable by the Appellant to the Plaintiff-Respondent.
