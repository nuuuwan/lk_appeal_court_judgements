Grounds of Review and Analysis As submitted in the Petitioners’ final consolidated written submissions, the central issue in the instant applications is whether the IRD could have lawfully invoked and exercised the power of rejecting the aforementioned returns submitted by the Petitioners (P3) and issuing Notices of Assessment (P4) in terms of section 163(3) of the IRA 2006 in the year 2022, nearly five years after the said IRA 2006 had been repealed by the IRA 2017. The Petitioners contend that the IRD could not have done so, and as such, the decisions reflected in P3, P4, P5 and consequently P8 are illegal and ultra vires. Prior to delving into a detailed analysis, it would be helpful to provide an overview of the cases presented by the parties. In their pleadings and submissions, the Petitioners state that they had made a self-assessment of their tax liability as provided in section 113 of the IRA 2006 in respect of the Y/A 2016/2017 after the Court-sanctioned arrangement and submitted their Returns of Income before the prescribed date. They contend that by filing their self-assessment Returns and paying the tax due thereunder, they had discharged their tax liability for the said year of assessment, and, also that their substantive tax liability became discharged and extinguished as a matter of law, at that point in time. The Petitioners further contend that, it was open for the IRD to accept or reject the returns submitted by them and to issue Assessments in terms of section 163(3) of the IRA 2006

a. The Accrual and Duration of the Petitioners’ Tax Liability It is recalled that the position taken up by the Petitioners is that their tax liability in respect of the Y/A 2016/2017 was discharged when they filed their returns and paid the tax due thereunder, and, that the issuance of the impugned Notices (P4), (P5) and (P8) constituted an “act and event,” which created “a fresh, new and independent liability” in 2022, after the repeal of IRA 2006.3 The issuance of such Notices were distinct juridical acts which altered the Petitioners’ legal rights in a fundamental manner by exposing them to much greater liabilities, triggering rights of objection and appeal and enabled coercive recovery mechanisms. However, it is observed that, although certain preliminary grounds relating to jurisdiction and time bar have been taken up in their appeals (P6), this objection has not been articulated therein in specifically in the same manner. Nevertheless, the Respondent has contested the said position taken up by the Petitioner.4 The response of the Respondent is that the “year of assessment” is the only basis on which income tax is calculated, and recovered under IRA 2006 and IRA 2017 as well. In such circumstances, the only “event” which occurred in the instant applications was the submission of the returns in respect of such year of assessment by the Petitioners on 27.11.2017 (Melstacorp) and 22.11.2017 (DCSL) respectively in relation to their tax liability for the Y/A 2016/2017. The Petitioners in turn has rejected such construction submitting that it was “wholly untenable in law and specious in the background of the self-assessment statutory scheme.”5 A convenient starting point to examine the respective arguments presented by the parties would be examine how income tax liability arises. On this issue, the Respondents drew the attention of this Court the early English case W H Cockerline & Company v. Commissioners of Inland Revenue,6wherein the explanation made by Lord Dunedin in Whitney v. Commissioners of Inland Revenue7 was cited with approval by Lord Hanworth MR in the following terms;

Now, there are three stages in the imposition of a tax: There is the declaration of liability, that is the part of the statute which determines what persons in respect of what property are liable. Next, there is the assessment. Liability does not depend upon assessment. That, ex hpothesi, has already been fixed. But assessment particularizes the exact sum which a person liable has to pay. Lastly, come the methods of recovery if the person taxed does not voluntarily pay. (emphasis added) Lord Hanworth further observed that:

'...but the charge is made, in consequence of the Act, upon the subject; the assessment is only for the purpose of quantifying it. (emphasis added)

Accordingly, the learned Deputy Solicitor General for the Respondent submitted that the position taken up by the Petitioners that a new and independent tax liability accrues from an assessment made in relation to the returns submitted by the Petitioners is contrary and repugnant to such established position of the law.8 The structure of the law becomes more evident from the provisions governing the submission of returns contained in section 106 of IRA 2006, which are as follows;

106.(1) Every person who is chargeable with income tax under this Act for any year of assessment shall, on or before the thirtieth day of November immediately succeeding the end of that year of assessment, furnish to an Assessor or Assistant Commissioner, either in writing or by electronic means, a return in such from and containing such particulars as may be specified by the Commissioner-General, of his income, and if he has a child, the income of such child: …… (emphasis added) The aforementioned section 106(1) imposes an obligation upon “every person who is chargeable with income tax” to submit a Return of Income on or before 30th November immediately succeeding the end of the year of assessment, and, it appears that the Petitioners have submitted their Returns in respect of Y/A 2016/2017 in compliance with such provision. It is evident from the text of section 106(1), inter alia, that “chargeability” or “accrual” of the tax liability has arisen prior to the submission of the return, and, in fact, is it such fact that necessitates the submission of the return. In the instant case, it is observed that the Letters of Intimation (P3) by the IRD make specific reference to the returns submitted by the Petitioners and contain reasons for the inability to accept the same. Thus, it is evident that the Letters of Intimation (P3) and the consequent Notice of Assessment (P4) and the Penalty Notices (P5) have been issued in respect of and in response to the aforementioned returns submitted by the Petitioners. In fact, the Respondent has taken up the position that, if the Petitioners’ argument is to be accepted, the “event” should be the return and the year of assessment for the tax payable and not the assessment since all Assessments are based and generated on the taxable amount for the year of assessment and based on the return, where one has been filed as in the instant case.9 Considering the statutory scheme set out in set out in IRA 2006 (and even IRA 2017) relating to the collection of taxes initiated by self-assessment, I am inclined to accept such position taken up by the Respondents. I am also unable to accept the Petitioners’ contention that their tax liability in respect of the Y/A 2016/2017 was discharged when they filed their returns and paid the tax due thereunder. This is because, as conceded by the Petitioners themselves, it is open for the IRD to either accept or reject the said return and to issue an assessment in instances where the return is rejected. Such fact alone is adequate to demonstrate that the filing of a return is neither the starting point of tax liability or its point of discharge and that there is no finality attached to

In such context, the filing of a return can be best described as an intermediate point where the tax payer notifies the IRD of his/her self-assessed tax liability in respect of a particular year of assessment, whereupon the IRD has the discretion to either accept or reject same and proceed to make an assessment. In view of the foregoing, I am inclined to accept the submission made by the learned Deputy Solicitor General that such acts constitute sequential phases or steps within a single process initiated by the submission of the Return and are not independent or stand-alone processes. As such, I am unable to accept the contention of the Petitioners that the issuance of the impugned Notices constituted and established a fresh and new liability in 2022 that was independent of the Returns submitted by the Petitioners in November 2017. b. Legal Regime governing the Petitioners’ Tax Liability Based on their characterization of the issuance of the impugned Notices as an “event” that occurred after the repeal of IRA 2006, are not governed by the said Act and the issuance of same thereunder is ultra vires. Whilst I have determined above that the issuance of the impugned Notices did not constitute or establish “a fresh, and new and independent liability” in 2022, it is nevertheless necessary to ascertain whether the issuance of the impugned Notices and the action contemplated thereunder are valid in law. On this issue, the broad position of the Petitioners is that, since the impugned Notices were issued after the repeal of IRA 2006, and, are not saved by the repealing IRA 2017, the latter Act is applicable. On the contrary, the Respondent has taken up the position that, since the dispute related to the tax chargeable for Y/A 2016/2017 and the Returns in respect of same were filed whilst IRA 2006 was in operation, the provisions of the said Act are applicable to the impugned Notices by virtue of the savings made in the repealing IRA 2017. Central to the arguments presented by both parties are sections 202, which relates to “repeal and savings” and 203(1), which sets out transitional provisions, of IRA 2017. The Respondent has further submitted that the language in such sections is plain, concise and clear and does not require any further statutory interpretation. I am inclined to accept the above position taken up by the Respondent. In my view the said section 202(2) read with section 203(1) of IRA 2017 unequivocally and unambiguously expresses the intention of Parliament to permit the continued application of IRA 2006 in respect of “years of assessment” commencing prior to 01.04.2018, which was the date on which IRA 2017 came into effect. In the circumstances, I am of the view that, notwithstanding its repeal, the tax liability of the Petitioners in respect of Y/A 2016/2017 have to be determined in terms of IRA 2006, and, as such, the Respondent has correctly issued the impugned Notices in terms of IRA 2006. Citing sections 202(5) and 202(6) of IRA 2017, the Petitioners have also advanced the argument that the express preservation of proceedings already commenced prior to repeal necessarily excludes the commencement of new proceedings under the repealed enactment after its repeal.15 However, in view of the determination that the issuance of the impugned Notices did not constitute or establish a fresh and new liability in 2022 that was independent of the Returns submitted by the Petitioners in November 2017, such argument would not be applicable to the facts and circumstances of the instant case. The same Division of this Court was confronted with fairly similar factual circumstances in Oru Mix Asphalt Pvt Ltd v. W.A. Sepalika Chandrasekera, Commissioner General of Inland Revenue and another.16 The said application also dealt with tax liability in respect of Y/A 2016/2017 and the petitioner sought to impugn the issuance of a Certificate of Tax in Default and the institution recovery proceedings instituted in the Magistrate’s Court of Colombo in June 2024 relying on the provisions of IRA 2006. The petitioner had failed to submit a return within the stipulated period, although a belated return had been submitted on 22.04.2021. As in the instant case, the assessment, the notice of tax in default and the Certificate of Tax in Default had been issued and the recovery proceedings instituted under the provisions of IRA 2006, after the repeal of the said Act. The argument presented by the petitioner was that the Certificate of Tax in Default was issued and recovery action instituted under the repealed IRA 2006 was bad in law, and, that, action, if any, should have been taken under IRA 2017 which had repealed and replaced the said Act by the time the impugned action by the IRD was taken. Although the petitioner in the said case and the Petitioners in the instant case may have invoked the jurisdiction of this Court at different stages of the revenue collection process, the central issue and the matter for determination in both applications was the legality of action taken by the IRD under IRA 2006 after its repeal and replacement by IRA 2017. used. The words used are “may be recovered” which allows the Inland Revenue Department to use the mechanism stated under Act, No. 10 of 2006 or Act, No. 24 of 2017 to commence recovery proceedings. The Act does not state so.
