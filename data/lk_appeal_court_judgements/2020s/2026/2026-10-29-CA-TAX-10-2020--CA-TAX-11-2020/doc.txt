ANALYSIS

In considering the Questions of Law raised by the Appellant in CA/TAX/10/2020 and

CA/TAX/11/2020, I identify two main issues for determination.

Questions of Law No. I, II, III, IV and V relate to the validity of the assessments issued by the

Assistant Commissioner and therefore will be considered together. Questions of Law No. VI to

XVIII concern the application of the exception under section 13(ddd) of the Inland Revenue Act

No. 10 of 2006 (IRA 2006). Hence, I shall consider these questions separately from the issue of

the validity of the assessments.

Validity of the Assessment

It is the contention of the Appellant that the assessment issued is not valid in law for the

following reasons;

a. That the Assistant Commissioner is not empowered to issue an assessment for the Year of

Assessment 2011/2012.

b. The intimation letter and the notice of assessment were not issued by the same person.

c. The intimation letter did not require the reasons for the rejection of the taxable return.

It is the first contention of the Appellant that prior to the Inland Revenue (Amendment) Act No.8

of 2014 coming into effect on 1st April 2014, an assessment can only be raised by an Assessor

and not by an Assistant Commissioner of the Inland Revenue. On this basis the Appellant argues

that the assessment is not valid in law.

Page 14 of 28

As the law stood prior to the Amendment Act No.8 of 2014, the section 163(3) of the IRA 2006

reads as follows;

“163.

(3) Where a person has furnished a return of income, the Assessor may in making an

assessment on such person under subsection (1) or under subsection (2), either–

(a) accept the return made by such person; or

(b) if he does not accept the return made by that person, estimate the amount of

the assemble income of such person and assess him accordingly:

Provided that where an Assessor does not accept a return made by any person for any

year of assessment and makes an assessment or additional assessment on such person for

that year of assessment, he shall communicate to such person in writing his reasons for

not accepting the return.”

Section 163(3) of the Inland Revenue Act No. 10 of 2006 empowers an Assessor to assess a

person who has furnished a return and, where the return is not accepted, requires the reasons for

such rejection to be communicated in writing. However, in the present matter it is the Assistant

Commissioner, who has communicated the reasons for not accepting the taxpayer’s return by the

letter of intimation dated 15th of December 2014.

It is the contention of the Respondent that the Inland Revenue (Amendment) Act No.8 of 2014

empowers the ‘Assessor’ as well as the Assistant Commissioner; therefore, the Assistant

Commissioner has the authority to issue an assessment. However, it is pertinent to note that the

assessment in dispute is for the taxable period 2011/2012 and the Inland Revenue (Amendment)

Act No.8 of 2014 came into effect only from 1st April 2014 and it cannot be applied in the

present instance.

Page 15 of 28

The section 163(3) of IRA 2006 requires the only Assessor to state the reasons for rejecting the

return, and not merely the conclusion reached. It needs to be noted that the IRA 2006, does not

provide a wide interpretation for the term ‘Assessor’. Therefore this court can only determine

that it’s the duty of the Assessor alone to issue an Assessment. Even though, there has been a

lacuna in terms of this, the amendment made on 2014 clearly rectify this by providing Assessor

as well as the Assistant Commissioner the authority to issue an assessment.

Therefore, when considering the above provision, the wording refers the Assessor alone, to issue

an assessment. Accordingly, I am of the view that the Assistant Commissioner was not acting

within the authority conferred by Section 163(3) of the Act in issuing the assessment.

The second contention of the Appellant is that the letter of intimation and the corresponding

notice of assessment should be issued by the same person. However, Sections 163 and 164 of the

Inland Revenue Act, which deal with the assessment and the notice of assessment, respectively,

require the relevant functions to be carried out by an Assessor, but do not expressly require both

documents to be issued by the same person. In the absence of such a requirement, I am of the

view that the fact that the letter of intimation and the notice of assessment were issued by

different officers does not, by itself, render the assessment invalid. It would also be unreasonable

to impose such a requirement on the Department of Inland Revenue, which is responsible for

administering tax liabilities throughout the country, in the absence of a clear statutory basis for

doing so.

The third contention of the Appellant is that the intimation letter issued by the Assessor does not

connote the reasons for the said rejection. In line with such argument it is important to

understand the context of such rejection.

The Assistant Commissioner of the Inland Revenue, in the letter dated 15th of December 2014 in

the case of CA/TAX/10/2020, has mentioned the following in rejecting the tax return furnished

by the Appellant.

Exception under section 13(ddd) of the IRA 2006

It is the contention of the Appellant that it is entitled to the exception provided under section

13(ddd) of IRA 2006.

In analyzing this substantive issue raised, it is important to understand the relevant in relation to

that. The section 13 of the IRA 2006, deals with the miscellaneous exemptions from income tax.

Accordingly, section 13(ddd) reads as follows;

“13. There shall be exempt from income tax—

(ddd) the profits and income earned in foreign currency by any resident company, any

resident individual or any partnership in Sri Lanka, from any service rendered in or

outside Sri Lanka to any person or partnership outside Sri Lanka, other than any

commission, discount or similar receipt for any such service rendered in Sri Lanka, if

such profits and income (less such amount, if any, expended outside Sri Lanka as is

considered by the Commissioner-General to be reasonable expenses) are remitted to Sri

Lanka through a bank,”

1A. D.M.S. Fernando and Another v. Mohideen Ismail [1982] 1 SLR 222

Page 20 of 28

The section 13(ddd) of IRA 2006, sets out certain requirements for the exception under the said

provision to be applied.

a. profits and income earned in foreign currency by any resident company.

b. from any service rendered in or outside Sri Lanka to any person or partnership outside

Sri Lanka.

c. remitted to Sri Lanka through a bank.

d. other than any commission, discount or similar receipt for any such service rendered in

Sri Lanka.

The above provision requires four elements to be satisfied for the exception to apply. In the

present matter, the first three elements are not in dispute. Both parties accept that the Appellant

earns profits in foreign currency from services rendered to clients outside Sri Lanka and that the

payments are remitted to Sri Lanka through a banking channel.

The dispute concerns the fourth element, which requires the payment received to be in the nature

of a commission, discount or similar receipt for services rendered. The Respondent contends that

the Appellant acts as an agent for its principal, namely, the DB Schenker Logistics Network

Group of Germany, and therefore the payments received for the services rendered are

commissions arising from that agency relationship.

The Appellant, however, maintains that it carries on the business of providing freight forwarding

services to foreign freight forwarders nominated by overseas customers. It further contends that

it contracts directly with those foreign freight forwarders, issues invoices to them and receives

payment directly from them in foreign currency. The Appellant further argues that it has not

claimed the exception in respect of the separate ‘Network Contribution Fee’ payable under the

DB Schenker Network arrangement. It also contends that, in relation to export transactions, the

Network Contribution Fee is paid by the Appellant. In support of its position, the Appellant

Page 21 of 28

relies on the cases of CGIR V. Aitken Spence Travels Limited2and CGIR V. Jetwing Travels (Pvt)

Ltd3.

The TAC in its determination dated 20th of February 2020, has held in the following manner;

“As referred to above, the Appellant is involved in a business of freight Network Group

of Germany, in Sri Lanka. It is confirmed from the notes of the forwarding and acts as

freight forwarding agent, on behalf of DB Schenker Logistic financial statements for the

year ended 31st March 2012, item 1.2, under the heading "Principal Activities and

Nature of Operations" which states that "During the year, the principal activities of the

Company were freight forwarding. The company acts as a freight forwarding agent of

DB Schenker Logistic Network Group of Germany, in Sri Lanka." The Appellant

company also pays a network connection fee to Schenker AG for being part of that

network. The term "Affiliates" referred to are business concerns, organizations or

individuals that control each other or that are controlled by a third party. The power to

control is the key factor in affiliation with another business, whether excised or not. In

the present case, the power to control lies with Schenker AG of Germany, the owner of

the network. Further, it is confirmed from the provisions of the agreement entered in to

with the Appellant, SG Logistics (Pvt) Ltd.

The item No. 3 of the agreement under the heading "Rate of Network Contribution"

which reads as follows: - "The current rate of the Network Contribution is EUR 0,045

per kg of the Chargeable Weight. This rate may be modified by Schenker AG. SG

Logistics (Pvt) Ltd. agrees that the rate set by Schenker AG shall be applicable to all

business between SG Logistics (Pvt) Ltd. and the Affiliates". Therefore, it would appear

that there is no relationship of principal and agent or employer and employee

relationship with the affiliates. The affiliates are controlled by Schenker AG of Germany,

who will be their principal. This is further seen from article 12.1 of the agreement which

states that, "SG Logistics (Pvt) Ltd. (the Appellant) shall comply with the operating

procedures and the regulations of Schenker as laid down in Annex C to Annex G and all

other operating procedures and regulations of Schenker in connection with the services

and the transportation of goods as may be promulgated by Schenker from time to time".

Therefore, it is clear that the Appellant company is paid a rate of network charge based

on the weight of the goods exported. Further, it would appear that the Appellant

received charges for the service rendered as an agent of DB Schenker of Germany.

Hence, it is an obvious fact that, the persons who rendered services as agents receive

commission, discounts or similar receipts for their services. Section 13 (ddd) of the

Inland Revenue Act, specifically excludes the application of the exemption granted by

that section, when the section clearly states that "other than any commission, discount or

similar receipt for any such service rendered in Sri Lanka".

In this case, we have carefully considered the submissions made by the Representatives

for the Appellant and the Respondent. Having regard to the material referred to above, it

is clear that the Appellant is not entitled to the exemption claimed under Section 13 (ddd)

of the Inland Revenue Act, as amended. Hence, we confirm the determination of the

Respondent and dismiss the appeal of the Appellant.” [emphasis added]

Having considered the notes submitted by the parties and the nature of the transactions, the TAC

has refused to grant the exception claimed by the Appellant under section 13(ddd). The TAC

proceeded on the basis that the Appellant was acting as an agent of DB Schenker of Germany

and that the payments received by the Appellant were therefore in the nature of commissions

arising from that agency relationship.

The Appellant, however, disputes this position. The Appellant acknowledges that its audited

financial statements described the company as a ‘freight forwarding agent of DB Schenker of

Germany’. Nevertheless, the Appellant contends that this description was subsequently corrected

by the same auditors, and that the relevant documents evidencing such corrections were

submitted to the TAC (vide A5(i)). Accordingly, the Appellant argues the correction made to the

description in the audited financial statements should have been considered in arriving at the

decision.

Page 23 of 28

The TAC, in its determination, has merely concluded that the Appellant receives commission on

the basis that it is an entity under DB Schenker, without referring to any supporting material

evidence. Nevertheless, it must be taken into account that, even assuming that an agency

relationship exists between the parties, the fact that the Appellant receives its income by way of

commission must be established by evidence. Assuming that there is a mere existence of an

agency relationship does not necessarily indicate that the income received is in the form of

commission. Even where such a relationship exists, the income may be paid through other

means, including profit-sharing arrangements, as agreed upon by the parties. Hence, I am of the

view that, even assuming that there is a mere existence of an agency relationship does not, by

itself, justify the conclusion reached by TAC that the income received by the Appellant

constitutes commission.

Furthermore, the TAC exercising an administrative function cannot merely come to a conclusion

without legal basis and material evidences, and the Principles of Natural Justice has to be

followed in process of such determination.

Having considered the foregoing, I am of the view that that TAC in arriving at its determination,

has failed to substantiate its conclusion with sufficient evidence.

Furthermore, in determining the services rendered by the Appellant and the subsequent payment

made, the required agreement between the parties needs to be considered. Accordingly, the

‘International Partner Agreement’ entered by the Appellant with DB Schenker of Germany needs

to be taken into account.

I shall reproduce the required clauses of the said agreement for reference below;

“Article 3 - Authority

In the performance of the Services, each party acts in its own name and on its own

account. Unless authorised in writing, neither party shall have the authority to enter into

Agreements binding on the other party or to bind the other party in any other way.

Article 9 - Remuneration

9.1. 1 A remuneration for the Services shall be paid by one party to the other in accordance with 9.1.1. Annex C in case of shipments by air, 9.1.2. Annex D in case of Full-Container-Load shipments by sea, 9.1.3. Annex E in case of Less-than-Container-Load shipments by sea, 9.1.4. (where applicable: Annex F in case of international transport by road). In case of Services other than those specified in Annexes C, D, E or F, the remuneration 9.2. shall be in the form of a profit share on a 50/50 percent basis, unless the parties agree otherwise.

Article 3 of the agreement provides that, in performing the services each party shall act in its

own name and on its own account. Further, Article 9, which deals with remuneration for the

services rendered, provides that subject to the specific terms set out in Annexures C, D, E and F,

the remuneration shall be based on a 50:50 profit-sharing arrangement. None of the said

Annexures characterises such payments as commissions or otherwise indicates that the

remuneration is received by the Appellant in that capacity (vide A9).

Having considered the terms of the agreement as a whole, I am of the view that the arrangement

between the Appellant and DB Schenker does not establish that the Appellant receives

commission income from DB Schenker. Accordingly, the material before this Court does not

indicate that the payments received by the Appellant for the services rendered constitute

commission income.

In these circumstances, I am of the view that the TAC has failed to give due consideration to the

material documentary evidence, particularly the terms of the agreement between the Appellant

and DB Schenker, in determining the nature of the relationship between them. Consequently, the

conclusion that the Appellant received commissions by virtue of an agency relationship is not

supported by the terms of the agreement.

For these reasons, I hold that the payments received by the Appellant are not in the nature of

commissions or similar receipts within the meaning of section 13(ddd).

As the first three requirements of the said provision are undisputed and the fourth requirement

has also been satisfied, hence I am of the view that the Appellant has fulfilled all the

requirements for the application of the exception under section 13(ddd).

Accordingly, I answer the substantive Questions of Law in favour of the Appellant.
