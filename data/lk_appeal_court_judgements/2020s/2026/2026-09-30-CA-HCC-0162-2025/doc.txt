11. A material contradiction refers to a discrepancy in statements or facts

that undermines credibility of the information presented. In contrast, a

material omission involves the intentional, or unintentional withholding

of significant information, that could alter the understanding of a

situation.

10

12. Material contradictions occur when two or more statements regarding

the same fact or event are logically inconsistent. For instance, if a

witness provided conflicting accounts of an incident these

contradictions may undermine his testimony. This can lead to

questions about witness’s reliability and ultimately, affect the outcome

of a case.

13. On the other hand, material omissions are equally significant. These

occur when pertinent information is deliberately left out, or

unintentionally disregarded. The interplay between contradictions and

omissions is crucial and can severely impact the integrity of the

information and by extension the decisions made based on that

information.

14. As adverted to above, PW01 has testified to incidents of intercrural sex

and anal penetration involving the appellant. However, when providing

information to Dr. Dissanayaka, PW01 has made no mention of anal

penetration. A discrepancy, therefore, exists between the two accounts.

Since the allegation of anal penetration relates to the incident forming

the subject matter of the second charge, this discrepancy is material.

The Material discrepancy in turn undermines the reliability of PW01’s

testimony.

15. Consequently, as a matter of prudence, the Court would seek

corroboration of PW01’s testimony to satisfy itself as to its truthfulness.

However, no such corroboration is forthcoming. Dr. Dissanayaka,

recording the nature, size, shape, disposition and the site of injury has

made a record as follows; “No evidence of anal penetration” (Vide page

15 of the Brief)

11

16. In the case of Ajith vs Attorney General [2009] 1 Sri. LR 23, Sisira de

Abrew J, has held as follows;

“I hold that it is very dangerous to act on the uncorroborated testimony

of a victim of a sexual offence. However, if the Court can without any

hesitation, accept and believe that the story narrated by her is true then

the Court can act on such evidence even without corroboration. I would

like to state here that Court, in cases of rape, as a rule of prudence

normally looks for corroboration of her testimony in order to satisfy its

conscience that she is telling the truth.”

17. Further, in the case of Premasiri vs. Attorney General [2006] 3 Sri. LR

106, Eric Basnayake J, has held as follows;

“The rule is not that corroboration is essential before there can be a

conviction in a case of rape, but the necessity of corroboration as a

matter of prudence, except where the circumstances make it unsafe to

dispense with it, must be present to the mind of the judge.”

18. As such, the learned High Court Judge has misdirected herself in

concluding that PW01 was consistent in his testimony and therefore, a

reliable witness.

19. Due to the matters referred to above, I am inclined to interfere with the

conviction, the disputed judgement together with the sentencing order.

20. I acquit the appellant of the first and second charges and proceed to set

aside the disputed judgement together with the sentencing order.
