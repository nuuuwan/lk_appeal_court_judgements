12] I now consider the merits of the case. Article 13 (5) of the Constitution (Every

person shall be presumed innocent until he is proved guilty: Provided that the

burden of proving particular facts may, by law, be placed on an Accused person)

norms that the court should draw the presumption of innocence until guilty of

the Accused is proved and this provision enriched by Article 13 (3) (Article 13(3)

Any person charged with an offence shall be entitled to be heard, in person or

by an Attorney-at-Law, at a fair trial by a competent court.) enabling the right

to represent by an attorney at law and by giving fair trial. The golden rule in

criminal law is that the burden is on the Prosecution to prove guilty of the

Accused. Thus, the ingredients of the charge must be proved.

13] in proving a fact, “Proved” or “Disproved” of a fact is defined in Section 3 of

the Evidence Ordinance. It defines what is meant by proved and disproved. It

says;

“A fact is said to be proved when, after considering all the matters before it,

the Court either believes it to exist or considers its existence so probable

7

that a prudent man ought, under the circumstances of the

particular case, to act upon the supposition that it exists.

A fact is said to be disproved when, after considering the matters before it,

the Court either believes that it does not exist, or considers its non-

existence so probable that a prudent man ought, under the

circumstances of the particular case, to act upon the supposition that

it does not exist. A fact is said not to be proved when it is neither proved nor

disproved.” [Emphasis is added]

14] Thus, the yardstick of existence of fact should be decided in the angle of

prudent man. The yardstick of the existence of a fact must be decided not by a

trained judicial mind such as judges or attorneys, must be by a prudent man

who is not a lunatic or mentally retarded who thinks in totality of evidence,

whether the fact is believed to be proved or not, whether the charge can be

sustained or not considering the totality of oral and documentary evidence which

came from the witness box; substantive evidence.

15] In Nandana Kumarage Sujeewa Nishanka Karunarathna vs Officer-in-

charge, Police Station, Rambukkana, SC APPEAL NO:

SC/APPEAL/61/2023, Decided on: 09/10/2024, His Lordship

Samayawardhena, J. held what is reasonable doubt as;

“The prosecution must prove the accused’s guilt beyond a “reasonable

doubt”, not beyond “any doubt”. A reasonable doubt refers to a doubt based

on logical reasoning through proper evaluation of evidence. This requires

the trier of fact to weigh all evidence supporting guilt against evidence

suggesting innocence, considering the strengths and weaknesses on both

sides. If, after this evaluation, the evidence overwhelmingly favors the

prosecution and eliminates any reasonable doubt about the guilt of the

accused, the case can be deemed proven beyond a reasonable doubt. This

standard applies to the totality of the evidence as a whole, not to each

individual piece of evidence the prosecution relies on to prove the guilt of the

accused.”

22] Are the above contradictions material? A material contradiction is a

significant and irreconcilable inconsistency in a Witness's statements or

evidence that goes to the core of a legal case and creates reasonable doubt. It

involves with central facts such as the identity of the Accused, the core action

of a crime, or vital elements of a dispute rather than minor, peripheral details.

Unlike trivial discrepancies or normal human memory lapses, a material

contradiction severely undermines the reliability and truthfulness of a Witness.

In a trial, proving a material contradiction can lead to the rejection of a Witness's

testimony and may result in an acquittal or a ruling in favour of the Accused.

23] In a material contradiction two statements flatly oppose each other on a

crucial point. There is a small variation in the details, such as a slight

miscalculation of time or a minor difference in description, which courts usually

accept as normal human fallibility. In this case all contradictions are involved

with major acts. The door was closed, but in the evidence, the door was open,

she felt a pain in the vagina and groin. But in evidence she said no pain was

caused in line with non-penetration. It should be noted that the evidence should

consider inter se and per se with other evidence. The Victim cannot be held as

a tender child who does not know the physical act. She was 12 years’ and had

already attended puberty at the time of the incident. The learned trial judge had

failed to consider the Accused was the sister’s partner and she had a child from

him. The Accused was underage and the sister did not consent to marry him.

The sister suspected the Accused had other affairs. However, the Accused

wanted to be with her. Thus, the Accused was pestering the sister. In her

mother’s statement, the sister was admitted to the mental hospital over these

incidents. Thus, she was not normal. Thus, her mental behaviour is volatile.

Further, the learned trial judge also failed to consider that the Victim told PW

10, the teacher who found her at the bus stand that she wanted to become a

11

sister. This is inter se contradiction between two witnesses. It is a fact, the

contradictions must be assessed, in view of the prudent man, not on the trained

judicial mind.

24] It is to be noted that the state must prove every element of a charge beyond

a reasonable doubt. If a contradiction shakes the core of the proof, a logical and

sensible doubt arises, the judge must give the Accused the benefit of the doubt

as a matter of right, leading to a not-guilty verdict.

25] In the case in hand, there was a single act, thus the Victim cannot be

jumbled with any previous sexual acts. If the Victim is subjected to continuous

or few more sexual assaults a prudent man can believe she may have jumbled

with a previous act. But if it is a single act, there cannot be any mess with the

evidence. Thus, I consider that the learned trial judge ought to have considered

the contradictions in favor of the accused in the back drop of the case, at her

conclusion. It is to be noted that direct evidence can be concocted without a

single contradiction when the Witnesses get together and make up a story. In

such circumstances, it is a trial judge’s bounded duty to consider the

background of the story. In this case, the scene of the crime was shown by the

මෙ ්පේවීම යට්ත් ්සායාෙත්තා.” The Accused was the paramour of Ruwani

26] In Ambika Prasad v State of New Delhi, 2000 SCC CRL 522, Indian

Supreme Court held;

“A criminal trial is meant for doing justice to the accused, victim and society.

So that law and order is maintained. A judge does not preside over a

criminal trial merely to see that no innocent man is punished. A judge also

presides to see that a guilty man does not escape. One is as important as

the other. Both are public duties.”

27] For the reasons stated above, I set aside the conviction and the sentence

The appeal is allowed.
