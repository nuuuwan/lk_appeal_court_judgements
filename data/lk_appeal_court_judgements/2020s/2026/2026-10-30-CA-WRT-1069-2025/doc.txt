Judgement

Introduction

According to the Islamic religion, it rests on five pillars. They are, the declaration of the faith in

one God, Allah, and that Prophet Muhammad is the last Prophet; the five daily prayers, known as

Salat; the giving of donations to the poor, known as Zakat; fasting during the holy month of

Ramadan; and, lastly, the pilgrimage to Mecca (Makkah), known as Hajj.

The pilgrimage made to Mecca (Makkah) is performed during the last holy month of the Islamic

calendar every year. The entire period of the pilgrimage covers almost one month, and every

person who professes the religion of Islam is expected to perform this pilgrimage at least once

during his lifetime, if he is physically and financially able to do so. Therefore, Hajj is one of the

five pillars upon which the religion of Islam rests.

The Petitioner claims that he is a travel agent, being a body corporate incorporated under the Laws

of Sri Lanka, and operates as a travel agent, with its main business being the organization and

operation of Hajj and Umrah pilgrimages to the Holy city of Mecca (Makkah). He claims that it

has been operating since the year 2018 and that, in fact, the 1st Respondent permitted and licensed

it to operate during the years 2022 and 2023 as a Hajj pilgrimage operator and an Umrah

pilgrimage operator.

Factual matrix

There is no statutory framework to regulate or deal with the operations of Hajj/Umrah pilgrimages.

15

There had been two Fundamental Rights applications bearing No. 345/2006 and 500/2012.

Pursuant to the directions given by the Supreme Court in the said applications in 2013, a set of

guidelines were prepared and implemented through the Muslim Religious and Cultural Affairs

Department; the said guidelines are so laid down and approved, and now implemented by the Hajj

Committee.

The said Committee comprises of the Director of the Department of Muslim Religious and Cultural

Affairs, along with certain other appointed members.

The 1st Respondent is the Director of the Department of Muslim Religious and Cultural Affairs,

whilst the 2nd Respondents is the Chairman of the Hajj and Umrah Committee of the Department

of Muslim Religious and Cultural Affairs (hereinafter referred to as the "Hajj Committee") and the

3rd to 6th Respondents are its members thereof. The 7th Respondent is the Minister of Buddhasasana,

Religious and Cultural Affairs, while the 8th and 9th Respondents are its deputy minister and

secretary, respectively. The 10th to 12th Respondents are Members of the Committee of

Independent Panel of Inquiry, appointed by the said Hajj Committee for a special purpose to look

into the complaints made against the Petitioner, while the 14th to 106th Respondents are persons

who were qualified to be called for the interview for the purpose of the issuance of licenses for

Hajj operations for the years 2025 and 2026.

Annually, the pilgrims are expected in Saudi Arabia to visit the Holy City of Mecca (Makkah) for

the purpose of performing Hajj and Umrah. Hajj is performed during the last month of every year

of the Islamic calendar, which is called 'Holy Month' or 'Hajj Month'. To regulate the operations

and provide proper accommodations and other necessary facilities, the Saudi Arabian Government

allocates visa quotas to different countries, depending on the relationship and standing of the

16

population professing in the religion of Islam of such country; thus, Sri Lanka also gets its annual

Hajj visa quota directly from the Saudi Arabian Government.

However, up until 2013, till the Supreme Court decided to give directions to set out certain

guidelines to regulate the pilgrimages arranged by the local travel operators, there had been no

guidelines or statutory provisions. Thus, the guidelines so set out pursuant to the directions given

by the Supreme Court in the said two Fundamental Rights Applications, now stands as the only

guidelines that fills the vacuum created in the background of the non-existence of any statutory

framework. Therefore, every travel operator has to follow the guidelines in pursuing applications

to obtain relevant quotas allotted to them by the Hajj Committee; this process starts after the last

month of the previous year's pilgrimage is over.

Thus, applications are called for, and after the applications are received, the applicants are called

for an interview or conference, on which, depending on their suitability and marks given to them,

the applicant travel operators are selected for the purpose of the allotment of their quotas; the said

marks are given according to the marking scheme given in Annexture IV to the said guidelines.

This includes;

• Registration (22 marks) - includes company registration, previous year Hajj license, tourist

board license, civil aviation, IATA license;

• Physical capabilities (19 marks) - includes active management with office premises, quality

of guide, efficient group leader, availability and quality of logistics, and medical facilities

• Financial capabilities (20 marks) - includes bank reference, proof of income tax payment,

strength of capital assets, and audit reports

• Experience (14 marks)

17

• Reliabilities (15 marks)

• Special facilities (10 marks) - includes having contingency funds, program for Hajj

orientation training, arrangement for training on ethics of congregation and social living,

grievance handling, and handbook on service delivery

Thereafter, once the allottee of quotas is selected, they are entitled to send the pilgrims to the Holy

City of Mecca (Makkah), and they are responsible for the ground operations and guidance of the

pilgrims in Mecca (Makkah), including the hotel accommodations and full coverage of the entire

month of Hajj during their pilgrimage.

When there are allegations against such operators, the Hajj Committee has the power to deal with

them.

The Respondents' Objections

The 1st, 8th, 9th, and 13th Respondents have filed their respective Statement of Objections; according

to which, they admitted that there is a guideline to regulate the Hajj Pilgrimage. However, they

deny the existence of guidelines to regulate the Umrah Pilgrimage.

Further, the said Respondents state that the said guidelines also contemplate the appointment of

separate independent panels to entertain complaints made by the pilgrims with regard to actual

facilities, accommodations, and other matters relating to the Pilgrimage.

Having said so, since the Petitioner had repeatedly committed delinquent acts on multiple

occasions, multiple complaints have been received by the Respondents, which justifiably banned

the Petitioner from organising Hajj tours.

Further, it is their position that due to the multiple complaints of fraud and failure to perform the

obligations as a travel operator to the pilgrims on Hajj tours organised by the Petitioner, compelled

the Respondents to take such drastic actions against him as well as to summon him before an

Independent Panel appointed under the Hajj guidelines to inquire into the allegations against the

Petitioner.

Having stated so, the said Respondents categorically deny any dereliction of duties, and any

irrationality, illegality, and ultra vires nature of their decisions taken; further, they state that their

decisions are only intra vires.

22

Arguments

The learned Counsel for the Petitioner, Ms. Dilumi De Alwis, argues that the Respondents have

failed to call the Petitioner for the interview for the purpose of issuing Hajj travel operator licenses

and quotas. Having referred to Rule 3 under the "The Hajj Travel Operator" of the Hajj Guidelines

marked as P2a, once an application is submitted, it is a must that the Hajj Committee calls such

an application for an interview, which the Respondents have failed, neglected and purposely

avoided in calling the Petitioner for such an interview for the issuance of licenses to operators for

the Hajj Pilgrimage 2026. Therefore, the Respondents have not only violated the rules of natural

justice, but also Articles 12(1) and 13(5) of the Constitution, where only some of the parties who

had submitted applications were called, excluding the Petitioner.

In addition to that, it is argued on behalf of the Petitioner that without even calling for an interview,

the names of the Umrah Pilgrimage operators of 2026 have been published; however, in the said

list of operators, the Respondents have failed, neglected or purposely avoided to mention the

Petitioner's name. Therefore, they have violated Articles 12 and 13 in this respect as well.

The next argument advanced on behalf of the Petitioner is that the Petitioner had legitimate

expectation to at least be called for an interview once the application was submitted to the

Respondent.

In those circumstances, it is argued on behalf of the Petitioner that since the Petitioner should have

at least been called for an interview prior to any rejection or making any other decision, not calling

the Petitioner for an interview is a matter that the Respondents have assumed to have discretion in

not calling the Petitioner, and therefore, had decided to shortlist the applicants who they should

23

call for an interview excluding the Petitioner. This itself shows that their decision not to call the

Petitioner for an interview is ultra vires.

It is also submitted that the Respondents have failed, neglected or purposely avoided to call the

Petitioner on flimsy reasons that there are allegations levelled against the Petitioner made by

certain pilgrims who had obtained the services of the Petitioner in the past. The Respondents have

assumed the discretionary power to decide not to call the Petitioner for interviews until those

pending cases are settled or concluded.

On the other hand, it is argued for and on behalf of the 1st, 8th, 9th, and 13th Respondents by the

learned Counsel Mr. Jayasinghe, D.S.G., that though the Hajj Guidelines is termed as 'guidelines'

set out for implementation by the Supreme Court, it is termed as "Hajj and Umrah Guidelines",

when in fact, there are no guidelines applicable for Umrah pilgrimages because performing of

Umrah occurs at any time other than for the Hajj Month, namely, the last month of the Islamic

calendar.

Furthermore, Mr. Jayasinghe argues that the fraudulent conduct of the Petitioner justifies the Hajj

Committee's decision not to call him for the interview.

Hajj rituals

During Hajj, rituals have to be continuously performed in Mecca (Makkah) for at least four days,

and there is a special dress code for the men and women to be worn during the performance of

Hajj. During the Holy Month, there is a huge competition among those who profess Islam to obtain

visas to visit the holy cities of Mecca (Makkah) and Medina; however, for the purpose of regulating

the accommodations and the performance of rituals, the Saudi Arabian Government has taken steps

to implement a quota system to different countries, depending on the relationship and Islamic

population standing; the performing of Umrah also depends on the time, funds, and facilities

available to such a pilgrim. As far as Hajj is concerned, there are a lot of rituals to be performed

in the pilgrimage, including paying homage to Kaaba, which is the holiest site in the Islamic world,

and the Hajj pilgrims have to visit a few other places to perform other rituals of the pilgrimage

including Ihram (dress code), Mina, Arafat, Muzdalifah, and Rami.

Accordingly, as referred to above, the Supreme Court had decided to prepare and issue the Hajj

Guidelines. However, there are no such compulsory guidelines for Umrah since it is only a

truncated pilgrimage and does not require such strict rituals to be performed. Hajj ends with the

throwing stones at the shaitaan represented by three special pillars. Thus, the performance of Hajj

is longer than Umrah.

Thus, though the guidelines in P2a is termed as the "Hajj and Umrah Guidelines"; therefore, if at

all, the Petitioner can only complaint that it has not been called for the interview with regard to the

issuance of visa quotas and operator licenses for the Hajj Pilgrimage.

Now I will consider whether the refusal to call the Petitioner for the interview is justified.

25

Justified decision of the Respondents

According to the Guidelines given, there is a certain marking scheme adopted. The Petitioner

claims he should have been called for the interview, even if that meant that he will be entitled to

receive only low marks at the interview. Nevertheless, the Petitioner has forgotten the fact that

there are serious allegations levelled against him, and based on those serious allegations, there are

several pending cases, and therefore, the Respondents have decided not to call the Petitioner for

the interview.

As mentioned above, Hajj is a solemn religious observance, where travel operators must have

proper logistical facilities arranged for when the pilgrims of a country to travel to Saudi Arabia for

the performance of Hajj. For that purpose, the travel operators must be able to mobilise all the

necessary ground support, and their agents in Saudi Arabia to have all the arrangements in place

for accommodations, transport, and all other logistical facilities, including the performance of the

rituals which includes Ihram (dress code), Mina, Arafat, Muzdalifah, Rami, Nahr (the sacrifice of

animals), Halaq and Taqsir (shaving and cutting of the hair), and Tawaf and Sa'ee (the

circumambulation and travelling back and forth around the Kaaba and between the Safa and

Marwah mounts), that occur at different locations including Mecca (Makkah), Mina Valley, Plain

of Arafat and Muzdalifah, and Jamarat.1

There are several complaints against the Petitioner, who had failed to provide the proper facilities

Having considered the fact that there are unresolved matters pending before courts, the

bad example for the future operators to follow suit.

On a perusal of the relief sought by the Petitioner as referred to above, most of them are in relation

to the Hajj Pilgrimage and for not calling it for the interview for the 2026 Hajj Pilgrimage; however,

the 2026 Hajj Pilgrimage is now also over, and therefore, calling it for an interview only becomes

an academic interest now. Therefore, I do not see any justification in granting such relief, even if

the Petitioner has good grounds.

Further, the second main relief that the Petitioner has sought is with regard to the Umrah

Pilgrimage. Though there is no proper procedure laid down for the regulation of the operations of

the Umrah Pilgrimage, by publishing the names of the qualified operators, the Respondents have

indicated to the world the names of the reliable operators who have not failed to perform their

duties as operators. Thus, there is no erroneous nature in publishing the said list. Therefore, the

actions taken by the Respondents are justified.

As such, to discourage such errant operators in the future, the Respondents have taken such drastic

measures, which is justifiable in this case as far as the Petitioner is concerned.
