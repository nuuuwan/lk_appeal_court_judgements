It is contended by the Respondent that, the two questions cannot

be raised in the case stated for the opinion of the Court of

Appeal since they are questions of fact and not questions of

law. The objections relates to the jurisdiction of the Court. It is

submissions belatedly, the same did not deal with the jurisdictional

C8.

Accordingly, the Court of Appeal may consider and determine any

Questions of Law arising on the stated case, upon which the

Court may confirm reduce, increase or annual the assessment.

Thus, it is clear that the Court of Appeal does not in any manner

have the jurisdiction to deal with the pure finding of fact of the

TAC. However, wrong analysis of the Question of fact is a Question

of Law.

In Collects Limited Vs. Bank Of Ceylon 2SLR 574, it has been

held that the improper analysis of fact is a Question of Law.

In this case before the Court, we do not have sufficient materials to

conclude that the TAC has come to an incorrect finding on this pure

question of fact. Therefore, the questions raised by the Appellant

will not amount to questions of law, granting jurisdiction to

become triable by this Court.

RESTRICTION

Judgment CA Tax No. 38/2019 Page 10|10

C9.

Question No 1 is in respect of observance of principle of natural

justice at the hearing before the CGIR. Question No 2 is in respect

of TAC’s conclusion on preliminary issue. There is not a point of law

involved in the two questions as those were framed. As the

questions raised in the case stated are not questions of law and as

per Section 11A of the TAC Act and Section 170 (1) proviso and 170

(6) of IRA No. 10 of 2006, this Court has no jurisdiction to try the

stated case.

D.

As analyzed above and as the two questions raised in the case

stated are not questions of law, this Court has no jurisdiction to try

the stated case and the same is REJECTED.
