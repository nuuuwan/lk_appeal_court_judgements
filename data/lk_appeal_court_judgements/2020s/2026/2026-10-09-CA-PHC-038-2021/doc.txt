K. M. S. DISSANAYAKE, J.

When this matter came on before us for argument on 30.09.2025, learned

Counsel for the Respondent-Petitioner-Respondent (hereinafter called and

referred to as “the Respondent”) raised a preliminary objection to Court as to the

maintainability of the instant appeal on the premise that, date of the judgement

sought to be impugned in the instant appeal had been wrongly, stated in the

prayer ‘b’ to the petition of appeal and as such, the instant appeal should be

dismissed in-limine.

Also, when this matter came on for hearing into the preliminary objection so

raised by the learned Counsel for the Respondent on 26.06.2026, the learned

Counsel for the Respondent raised a further preliminary objection as to the

amended petition of appeal filed in Court by the Applicant-Respondent-Appellant

(hereinafter called and referred to as “the Appellant”) on the premise that it had

been filed in Court by the Appellant without leave of Court first had and obtained

for that purpose and therefore, it should be rejected.

Counsel urged that, both preliminary objections be heard and determined on the

strength of the written submissions and accordingly, a consolidated set of

written submissions were furnished to Court by the respective parties. Hence,

the order on those two preliminary objections on the strength of the consolidated

written submissions, as urged and agreed by respective Counsel.

Both the preliminary objections would I think, call for separate treatment.

The date of the judgement sought to be impugned in the instant appeal had

been wrongly, stated in the prayer ‘b’ to the petition of appeal and as such,

appeal should be dismissed in-limine.

It may now, be examined.

The Appellant in the caption of the appeal appeared on page 2 of the appeal brief

had clearly, and unequivocally, stated in no uncertain terms the date

06.08.2021, as the date of the judgement sought to be impugned in the instant

appeal by him.

Furthermore, the Appellant had in paragraph 10 of the petition of appeal,

appeared on page 3 of the appeal brief too, stated 06.08.2021, as the date of the

judgement sought to be impugned in the instant appeal by him.

Moreover, the Appellant had in paragraph 11 of the petition of appeal, appeared

on page 3 of the appeal brief too, stated 06.08.2021, as the date of the judgement

sought to be impugned in the instant appeal by him.

Hence, the Appellant had not only, in the caption of the petition of appeal but in

the body of the petition of appeal also, clearly, and unequivocally, stated in no

uncertain terms and without any ambiguity, the date 06.08.2021, being the date

of the judgement that the Appellant had in the instant appeal, sought to impugn

before this Court.

Besides, upon a careful perusal of the judgement sought to be impugned by the

Appellant in the instant appeal which is filed of record, contained on pages from

133 to 142 of the appeal brief, it becomes abundantly, clear that, the date

inserted on the 2nd page of the judgement found on page 134 of the appeal brief

is the same date as stated in the caption as well as the body of the petition of

appeal as aforesaid, namely; 06.08.2021.

Furthermore, the date stated in the last page of the judgement appeared on page

142 of the appeal brief too, is 06.08.2021.

However, it appears on the face of the prayer ‘b’ of the petition of appeal that a

different date to that of the date inserted in the caption of the petition of appeal

as well as in the body of the petition of appeal as well as in the judgement

enumerated above, had been inserted in the prayer ‘b’ to the petition of appeal

as to the date of the judgement sought to be impugned by the Appellant in the

instant appeal, namely; 06.12.2017.

Hence, it clearly, appears that there is an obvious error in prayer ‘b’ of the

petition of appeal with regard to the manner in which the date of the judgement

sought to be impugned has been described therein.

However, upon a careful perusal of the petition of appeal and the judgement

sought to be impugned in the instant appeal in its totality, it clearly, appears the

error so occurred in describing the date of the judgement in prayer ‘b’ of the

petition of appeal is nothing more than a typographical error which can clearly,

and easily, be corrected with leave of the Court first had and obtained as provided

for by Rule 3(8) of the Court of Appeal (Appellate Procedure) Rules 1990, without

causing any prejudice to the Respondent and as held by this Court in Dias v.

Karawita 1999 [1] SLR 98 at Page 102.

Besides, when the Petition of appeal and the Judgement, sought to be impugned

are to be read in its correct perspective together with the erroneous prayer ‘b’ of

the petition of appeal, even without an amendment being made to the date

erroneously, stated in the in the prayer ‘b’ of the petition of appeal, this Court

can grant relief for; it manifestly, appears from a careful reading of the petition

of appeal together with the judgement sought to be impugned with the instant

appeal that, all what the Appellant had asked for in prayer ‘b’ of the petition of

appeal was to set aside the judgement delivered by the learned High Court Judge

of the Central Province holden in Nuwara Eliya on 06.08.2021.

Besides, it ought to have been within the full knowledge of Respondent that, it

was so for; the judgement sought to be impugned in the instant appeal had been

pronounced by the learned High Court Judge in the presence of both the

Appellant as well as the Respondent at a time where both of them had been duly,

represented by respective Counsel in Court.

Hence, it cannot in any manner, be said that the obvious error occurred in prayer

‘b’ of the petition of appeal, in describing the date of the judgement sought to be

impugned in the instant appeal, had caused any prejudice to the Respondent.

In view of all the above facts and circumstance, I would hold that, the preliminary

objection so raised by the learned Counsel for the Respondent is devoid of any

merit and as such, it ought to be overruled in-limine.

Amended petition of appeal had been filed in Court by the Appellant

without leave of Court first had and obtained for that purpose and

therefore, it should be rejected.

It may now, be examined.

It is to be observed that, subsequent to the preliminary objection being raised as

aforesaid by the learned Counsel for the Respondent, the Appellant had

furnished to this Court an amended petition of appeal explicitly, seeking in the

motion dated 10.10.2025, filed along with the proposed amended petition of

appeal, leave of this Court to amend the prayer ‘b’ of the petition of appeal

inserting only the correct date therein and nothing more apparently, under Rule

3(8) of the Court of Appeal (Appellate Procedure) Rules 1990.

Upon a careful perusal of the proposed amended petition of appeal in its totality,

it clearly, appears that, all what the Appellant had sought therein, is to amend

the date inserted in the prayer ‘b’ of the petition of appeal only and nothing more.

It appears to this Court that for the very reasons stated by this Court under the

first preliminary objection, no prejudice whatsoever, would be caused to the

Respondent by allowing the amendment sought therein, by the Appellant, for; it

ought to have been within the full knowledge of Respondent that, the judgement

sought to be impugned in the instant appeal which had been pronounced by

the learned High Court Judge in the presence of both the Appellant as well as

the Respondent at a time where both of them had been duly, represented by

respective Counsel in Court, had infact, been delivered by Court on the

06.08.2021.

Hence, it clearly, appears that, the next preliminary objection so raised as

aforesaid by the learned Counsel for the Respondent, too, is devoid of merit and

as such not entitled to succeed both in fact and law and as such, it too, should

be overruled in-limine.

In view of the foregoing, I would hold that, none of the preliminary objections is

entitled to succeed both in fact and law.

In the result, I would proceed to overrule both of the preliminary objections so

raised by the Respondent.

I would acting under Rule 3(8) of the Court of Appeal (Appellate Procedure) Rules

1990 thus, grant leave to the Appellant to furnish to Court an amended petition

of appeal.

In view of all the above, circumstances I make no order for costs.
