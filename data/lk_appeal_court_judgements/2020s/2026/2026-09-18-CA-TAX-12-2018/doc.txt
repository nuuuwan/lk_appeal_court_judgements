FACTUAL BACKGROUND

The Siam City Cement (Lanka) Limited, previously known as Holcim (Lanka) Limited

(hereinafter will be referred to as the Appellant) is a limited liability company incorporated

under the Companies Act No.17 of 1982 and domiciled in Sri Lanka. The principal business

activities of the Appellant is manufacturing, importing, selling, distributing, market

promoting and dealing with Cement and related products, articles made of cement, cement

concrete or other Cement mixtures.

The company has entered into an agreement with the Board of Investment (BOI) in 1998 and

the profit attributable by manufacturing of cement is exempt from income tax for a period of

12 years.

The Appellant has submitted the returns for the taxable periods 2004/2005 and 2005/2006

claiming certain deductions, which were rejected by the Senior Deputy Commissioner of the

Department of Inland Revenue by the intimation letter dated 29th of July 2010. Accordingly,

the Assessor has issued the Notice of Assessment dated 30th of March 2012 for the reasons

set out in the intimation letter.

The Appellant being aggrieved by the decision of the Assessor, made an appeal to the

Commissioner General of Inland Revenue (hereinafter will be referred to as the Respondent).

The Respondent in his determination dated 28th of April 2014 determined the appeal by

upholding the assessment issued by the Assessor. The Appellant being dissatisfied with the

said determination of the Respondent, made an appeal to the Tax Appeals Commission

(TAC) on the basis that the assessment issued by the Assessor is time barred and invalid. The

TAC after considering the submissions made by both parties made the determination dated

24th of April 2018 in favor of the Respondent.

Being dissatisfied with the said determination, the Appellant has requested the TAC to cause

a Case Stated to the Court of Appeal on the following Questions of Law.

ANALYSIS

The Question of law No. I is in relation to the time limitation in issuing an assessment under

the Inland Revenue Act No. 38 of 2000 as amended (IRA 2000) by the Assessor. Since this

matter goes to the root of the case, I shall address this issue at the outset. If it is answered in

the Affirmative, little purpose is served since the self assessment would be upheld and the

assessment issued by the Assessor will be quashed. Therefore, the rest of the questions of law

formulated by the Appellant shall not arise. Therefore, I intend to analyze the Question of law

No. I initially.

Question of Law No. I

The Appellant has made the assessments for the taxable periods 2004/2005 and 2005/2006

which were rejected by the Assessor on the basis that the deductions claimed for the royalties

and interest under section 29(2) of the IRA 2000 is not applicable to the Appellant while the

BOI agreement is in existence. Accordingly, the Appellant has sought to amend the return by

the letter dated 24th of March 2008 and the amended computation of the said assessments has

been submitted on 28th of March 2008. On this basis, the Assessor has issued the letter of

intimation dated 29th of July 2010, illustrating the reasons for the rejection of the assessments

made by the Appellant on 24th of March 2008. Subsequently, the Notice of assessment dated

30th of March 2012 was issued by the Assessor.

For the ease of reference, I shall provide the time lines for the year of assessments 2004/2005

and 2005/2006 in the following manner.

2004/2005 2005/2006

Initial return submitted 25th of November 2005 30th of November 2006

Amended Computation of the

assessment

24th of March 2008 24th of March 2008

Date of the letter of Intimation 29th of July 2010 29th of July 2010

Notice of Assessment 30th of March 2012 30th of March 2012

It is the contention of the Appellant that the Notice of assessment submitted by the Assessor

is time barred under section 134 of the IRA 2000, since the assessments have been made after

more than the three years of time period prescribed by law.

However, it is the Respondent’s position that the Appellant made the initial assessments for

the taxable years 2004/2005 and 2005/2006 within the required time limit. Subsequently, by

letter dated 24th of March 2008, the Appellant requested a refund and submitted amended

returns on the basis that certain deductions had not been made. Accordingly, it is the

Respondent’s position that, by submitting the amended returns for the taxable years

2004/2005 and 2005/2006 on 28th of March 2008, the Appellant has submitted the returns out

of time. Therefore, the Respondent contends that no return was furnished by the Appellant

within the prescribed time under section 134(5) of the IRA 2000, and hence, the time bar or

prohibition on making the assessment cannot be applied.

The Respondent further contends that the refund claim under section 169(3) of IRA 2000 can

only be applied if the errors in the returns furnished are purely clerical or arithmetic and not

due to the error in the interpretation of the application of the Act itself. Therefore, the

Respondent argues that the Appellant is not entitled for the refund claim.

When the appeal was before the TAC, the TAC having considered the submissions of both

the parties, have held in the following manner;

“The Representative for the Respondent submitted that, according to Section 169 (3)

of the Inland Revenue Act, the intention of the legislature is very clear that a person

who claims a refund should satisfy the Commissioner General that, the claim is made

in accordance with the law and the error in question was a human error. The

Representative referred to the Second paragraph of the letter dated 24.03.2008 sent

by the Appellant which reads as follows;

"The royalty paid for each of the years is included in the "Cost of Sales" and

"Administration Expenses" (Vide notes 3.2 & 7.5(f) for 2004/05, and note 7.5(e) for

2005/06) which [being a prohibited deduction under section 24(1)(m)] should have

Page 7 of 12

been added back in computing the statutory income from trade, but claimed under

Section 29 (2) (aaa) as a deduction from the total statutory income in arriving at the

assessable income."

It was submitted that, when perusing the above paragraph of the letter dated

24.03.2008, anyone can understand whether the error was a human error or error in

the application or construction of any provision of the Act. Only a person who has

understood the Section 23, Section 25 and Section 29 of the said Act, can make

adjustments as was done by the Appellant in this case. Further, it was submitted that,

it is evident from the time gap between the date of the submission of returns and

refund claim, the error that occurred was not a human error but an error that

happened due to the application or the construction of the provisions of the Act.

Therefore, in this case, the refund request required a fresh assessment by making

some adjustments in the calculation of the total statutory income. Hence, the

Representative for the Respondent submitted that the date of the submission of the

returns should be the date when the amendment was made and therefore the

Appellant was not entitled to the time bar protection under Section 134 (5) of the

Inland Revenue Act.

Section 169 (3) of the Inland Revenue Act reads as follows:

(3) Where it is proved to the satisfaction of the commissioner - General by

claim made in writing within three years of the end of a year of assessment

that any person has paid income tax, in excess of the amount which he was

liable to pay for that year of assessment and that excess is due to any error in

the assessment or the return of income, of that person (other than an error in

the application or construction of any provision of this Act, in the making or

revision of the assessment), such person shall be entitled to a refund of the

amount paid in excess.

In view of Section 169 (3) referred to above, it appears that, as submitted by the

Representative for the Respondent, the said provision permits only a correction of a

human error, other than an error in the application or construction of any provision

of the Act, in the making or revision of the assessment. Therefore, no amendment or

Page 8 of 12

revision of the assessment is permitted. In this case when one considers the contents

of the belated letter sent by the Appellant dated 24.03.2008, the Appellant was seeking

an amendment or a revision of the assessment. The said letter further states as

follows:

Year of Assessment Revised tax liability on taxable income

2004/2005 55,849,641

2005/2006 Nil

Therefore, it is very clear that the Appellant was seeking an amendment or revision to

the assessment. Under these circumstance, the Appellant cannot claim the time bar

benefit under section 134 (5) of the Inland Revenue Act.”

Having considered the contents of the letter dated 24th March 2008, the TAC has arrived at

the conclusion that the request for an amendment/revision was not permissible, on the basis

that the IRA 2000 does not provide for an amendment to be made to a return submitted by the

taxpayer. Accordingly, the TAC was of the view that the time bar under section 134(5) of the

IRA 2000 was not applicable to the Appellant.

Now I shall move onto analyze the significant provisions of the IRA 2000, which is

applicable to this matter.

The section 134(5) of the IRA 2000, sets out the time duration in which an assessor is

required by law to issue an assessment to the taxpayer when a return has been submitted.

“134.

5) Subject to the provisions of section 67. no assessment shall be made, of the income

tax payable under this Act -

Page 9 of 12

(a) by any person who has made a return of his income on or before the

thirtieth day of November of the year of assessment immediately succeeding

that year of assessment, after the expiry of the three years from the end of

that year of assessment; and

(b) by any person other than the person referred to in the preceeding

paragraph after the expiry of a period of six years from the end of that year of

assessment;";

Provided, that nothing in this subsection shall apply to the assessment of income tax

payable by any person in respect of any year of assessment, consequent to the receipt

by such person, of any arrears relating to the profits from employment of that person

for that year of assessment :”

According to section 134(5) of the IRA 2000, the Assessor is required by law to issue an

assessment on or before the expiry of the three years from the 30th day of November of the

immediately succeeding year of an assessment. Furthermore, the proviso to section 134(5)

emphasizes that the time bar cannot be applied only in a situation of fraud and willful default.

It needs to be noted that in the present instance, neither fraud nor willful default have been

claimed or established by the Respondent.

When considering the time frame of the events unfolded, it can be seen that the tax returns for

the year 2004/2005 has been submitted on 25th of November 2005 and the return for the year

2005/2006 on 30th of November 2006 which are within the required time line. Accordingly,

the letter of intimation has been issued dated 27th of July 2010, rejecting the amended

computation of the assessments made on 24th of March 2008. Subsequently, the notice of

assessment dated 30th of March 2012 has been issued by the Assessor.

Page 10 of 12

According to the time limitation mentioned under section 134(5) of IRA 2000, the Assessor

is required by law to issue the assessment within 3 years from the 30th day of November of

the immediately succeeding year of assessment. Therefore, the time to issue the assessments

for the returns are in the following manner;

Taxable year Assessment submitted on Time barred

2004/2005 25th of November 2005 29th of November 2008

2005/2006 30th of November 2006 29th of November 2009

Therefore, the Assessor is required to issue the assessment for the taxable year 2004/2005 on

or before 30th of November 2008 and the assessment for the taxable year 2005/2006 should

have been submitted on or before 30th of November 2009.

However, in the present matter, the assessments for the taxable years 2004/2005 and

2005/2006 have been issued by the Assessor on 30th of March 2012, which is well after the

three years of time period specified by law. Further, the Respondent has failed to provide any

significant reason for such an undue delay in computing the said taxable income of the

taxpayer.

Furthermore, this court is in line with the decision arrived by the TAC to the extent that the

IRA 2000 does not provide for specific provision for a tax return which was already

submitted to be amended. However, the TAC has gone wrong in deciding ultimately that the

Appellant is not entitled to invoke the time bar under section 134(5) of the IRA 2000.

It is my observation that, if the TAC had not considered the amended return dated 28th

March 2008, as there is no such provision permitting the submission of an amended return,

and should have disregarded the same, and the TAC should have proceeded to consider the

time bar on the originally submitted self-assessment. Consequently, this court is of the view

that the initial tax return submitted by the Appellant shall prevail. Therefore, the time bar for

the assessment issued for the taxable year 2004/2005 shall run from 30th of November 2005

Page 11 of 12

and will be barred on 29th of November 2008, and the time bar for the taxable year 2005/2006

shall run from 30th of November 2006 and will be barred on 29th of November 2009.

Furthermore, it needs to be noted that the Assessor has failed to issue an intimation letter in

relation to the initial assessment submitted by the Appellant. The intimation letter dated 29th

July 2010 relates only to the amended return dated 24th March 2008 and does not relate to the

original self-assessment. Subsequently, the Notice of Assessment dated 30th March 2012 was

issued, which was well after the time period prescribed by law. Therefore, as at that date, or

even up-to this date there was/is no intimation letter issued by the Assessor rejecting the

original self-assessment submitted by the Appellant.

Therefore, I am of the view that the TAC has erred in holding that the Appellant is not

entitled to invoke the time bar provision under section 134(5) of the IRA 2000. Hence, this

court holds that the Notice of assessment dated 30th of March 2012 is timed barred under

section 134(5) of the IRA 2000 as there has been a deliberate delay in issuing the assessment

by the Respondent.

Hence, I answer the question of law No.I in the Affirmative.

In light of the foregoing conclusion reached in the Question of Law No. I, I am of the view

that the remaining Questions of law raised by the Appellant will not arise and need not be

considered as the assessment dated of 30th of March 2012 is set aside and the self assessment

would prevail.
