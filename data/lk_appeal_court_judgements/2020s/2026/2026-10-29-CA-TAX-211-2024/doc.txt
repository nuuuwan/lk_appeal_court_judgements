FACTUAL BACKGROUND

The Ceylinco Insurance (Ceylinco Holdings) PLC (hereinafter will be referred to as the

Appellant) is a limited liability company incorporated and domiciled in Sri Lanka and listed

in the Colombo Stock Exchange. The principal activity of the Appellant is carrying on

Insurance business in Sri Lanka.

The Appellant has submitted the return for the taxable period 2014/2015, claiming certain

deductions, the Assistant Commissioner of the Department of Inland Revenue has not

accepted the said return (sometimes will be referred to as the Assessor) and has rejected the

said return for the reasons set out in the intimation letter dated 29th of May 2017.

Accordingly, the Assessor has issued the Notice of Assessment dated 30th of May 2017

based on the reasons set out in the intimation letter.

The Appellant being aggrieved by the decision of the Assessor, made an appeal to the

Commissioner General of Inland Revenue Department (hereinafter will be referred to as the

Respondent). The Respondent in its determination dated 10th of June 2019 determined the

appeal by upholding the decision of the Assessor. The Appellant being dissatisfied with the

said determination of the Respondent, made an appeal to the Tax Appeals Commission

ANALYSIS

The Appellant in the present matter has raised 24 Questions of Law before this court.

However, the preliminary questions in the present matter is in relation to the validity of the

assessment issued by the Assessor, since these questions goes to the very root of the case, I

shall initially address this issue. Therefore, I shall initially address Question of Law No. III,

and if the said question is answered in favour of the Appellant, little purpose is served in

considering the rest of the questions.

Question of Law No.III

It is the contention of the Appellant, that the taxable return for the period of 2014/2015 was

made on 29th of November 2015, and it was rejected by the Assessor for the reasons set forth

in the intimation letter dated 29th of May 2017. Thereafter the notice of assessment dated 30th

of May 2017 was served on the Appellant, which was received on 15th of June 2017. On this

basis, the Appellant argues that the assessment issued is time barred and therefore not valid.

However, it is the position of the Respondent that the time bar under section 163(5) of IRA

2006 is only subject to the act of ‘making an assessment’ as opposed to serving of ‘notice of

assessment’. Accordingly it is the Respondent’s position that the intimation letter containing

Page 8 of 13

the reasons for the rejection of the tax return has been served on the Appellant within the

required time frame, and therefore the assessment not time barred. In supporting their

position the learned State counsel for the Respondent Ms. Aluwihare relies on the cases of

Honig and Others V. Starsfield and the case of Stafford Motor Company (Pvt) Ltd V.

Commissioner General of Inland Revenue, [2019] 3 Sri LR 169.

When the Appellant had appealed to the TAC in the previous instance, the TAC in its

determination dated held that the assessment is not time barred for the reasons set out below;

“After analyzing the judgments of Stafford Motors Company and English case of

Honing and Others (Administers of Emanuel Honing) Vs Sarsfield (Inspector of

taxes), Dr. Ruwan Franando J. is of the view that time bar is on making the

assessment and not on sending the Notice of Assessment. Hence, in the case of

Ilukubura vs Industrial Automation (Pvt) Ltd, he further stated that "in my view the

results of these provisions in that the Court is not concerned here with the question of

the date. When Notice of Assessment was served. The Court is concerned with totally

different question, namely when were the assessment made? The giving of notice has

nothing to do with the making of valid and effective assessment".

Hence in view of the above analysis, hold that the assessment made in the instant case

is not made out of time and therefore not a nullity.

Further Dr. Ruwan Fernando J. in the latest case of CF Insurance Brokers (Private)

VS CGIR (CA/TAX/0040/2019) commented that," when addressing the question of

time bar, it could be seen that time bar provided in Section 163(5) applies to the

making of assessment". The word made in Section 163(5) (a) (1) and (11) b clearly

manifest this position.

It is important to note that No time bar for issuing of notice of Assessment, is set out

either in Section 164 or 163(1). If the intention of the legislature was to set up a time

bar for the issuing of notice of Assessment, the legislature had no difficulty in

enacting that no notice of Assessment should be given" after the expiry of the

prescribed time limit, instead of enacting that no assessment shall be made after the

Page 9 of 13

expiry of the prescribed time limit. Finally, an appeal was dismissed holding the view

that case is not made out of time and therefore not nullity. ”

The TAC in arriving at its decision has relied on the positions in certain judgments of this

court. However, it is significant to understand the current legal position of these aspects.

The section 163(5) of IRA 2006, which deals with the time limitation for the assessment

issued by the Assessor, reads in the following manner;

“163.

(3) Where a person has furnished a return of income, the Assessor or Assistant

Commissioner may in making an assessment on such person under subsection (1) or

under subsection (2), either–

(a) accept the return made by such person; or

(b) if he does not accept the return made by that person, estimate the amount

of the assessable income of such person and assess him accordingly:

Provided that where an Assessor or Assistant Commissioner does not accept a return

made by any person for any year of assessment and makes an assessment or

additional assessment on such person for that year of assessment, he shall

communicate to such person in writing his reasons for not accepting the return.

…

(5) Subject to the provisions of section 72, no assessment of the income tax payable

under this Act by any person or partnership –

(a) who or which has made a return of his or its income on or before the

thirtieth day of November of the year of assessment immediately succeeding

that year of assessment,

(i) where such year of assessment is any year of assessment

commencing prior to April 1, 2013, shall be made after the expiry of a

period of two years from the thirtieth day of November of the

immediately succeeding year of assessment; and

(ii) where such year of assessment is any year of assessment

commencing on or after April 1, 2013, shall be made after the expiry

of a period of eighteen months from the thirtieth day of November of

the immediately succeeding year of assessment:

(b) who has failed to make a return on or before such date as referred to in

paragraph (a), shall be made after the expiry of a period of four years from the

thirtieth day of November of the immediately succeeding year of assessment:

…

164.

An Assessor or Assistant Commissioner shall give notice of assessment to each person

and each partnership who or which has been assessed, stating the amount of income

assessed and the amount of tax charged:

…” [emphasis added]

According to section 163(5) of the IRA 2006, for an assessment commencing on or after 1st of

April 2013, it should be made within the expiry of eighteen months from the thirtieth day of

November of the immediately succeeding year of assessment. Therefore, since the

assessment in question is related to the taxable period 2014/2015, the assessor is required to

issue the assessment within eighteen months from the 30th of November 2015.

In considering the time limits mentioned above, the Assessor is required by law to furnish the

intimation letter and the Notice of Assessment on or before 30th of May 2017. However,

when considering the materials presented before this court, the stamp in the intimation letter

and the Notice of assessment indicates that the Respondent has received the said documents

only on 2nd of June 2017 and 15th of June 2017 respectively, which are well after the eighteen

months time period required by law.

Furthermore, it is pertinent to note that, in Fonterra Brands Lanka (Private) Limited V.

Commissioner General of the Department of Inland Revenue and Others (2025), SC Appeal

No. 187/2014, His Lordship Thurairaja J., having considered the previous judicial authorities

on the matter, declined to follow the English approach adopted in Honig V. Stransfield1 and

held that the term ‘assessment’ in Chapter XXII of the Inland Revenue Act No. 10 of 2006

refers not merely to a particular aspect of the assessment of a taxpayer’s liability, but to the

entire process of assessment.

In view of the aforesaid decision, this Court is not inclined to follow the position adopted in

Stafford Motors2, as the reasoning therein is founded on the English approach in Honig V.

Stransfield.

Although the Respondent has also relied on Illukumbura3, the sufficiency of the letter of

intimation in that case was considered in the context of whether the particulars contained

therein provided the taxpayer with a reasonable understanding of the tax liability in question.

The facts and circumstances of the present case, are distinguishable. Moreover, in Fonterra

Brands, His Lordship Thurairaja J., having considered the position taken in Illukumbura,

concluded that the making of an assessment cannot be separated from its communication to

the taxpayer. Unless and until the taxpayer is duly notified of the assessment, the assessment

process cannot be regarded as complete.

This Court, having regard to the decision of the Supreme Court in Fonterra Brands, has

adopted a similar approach in several previously decided cases, holding that the assessment

process is completed only when the assessment is communicated to the taxpayer by way of a

notice duly served within the time limit prescribed by law.

In the view of this Court, the term “assessment” as used in Section 163(5) cannot be confined

solely to the act of making an assessment by the Assessor. Alternatively, it encompasses the

entire process; including the determination of the taxpayer’s liability, the making of the

assessment, demanding the payment and the communication of that assessment to the

taxpayer. Accordingly, for the purposes of Section 163(5), it is not sufficient for the Assessor

merely to make or prepare an assessment within the prescribed period; the assessment must

also be duly communicated to the taxpayer within that period. If we are to give any contrary

interpretation, it will only enable the Assessor to keep the printed assessment containing the

quantum of the tax liability in their lockers indefinitely and it would defeat the purpose of the

statutory time limit and undermine the effective operation of Section 163(5) of the IRA

2006.4

Therefore, I am of the view that the TAC has erred in holding that the assessment issued by

the assessor is valid in law.

Hence, I answer the Question of Law No. III to the effect that the determination of the TAC

is erroneous.

In light of the foregoing conclusion reached in the Question of Law No. III, I am of the view

that the remaining Questions of law raised by the Appellant will not arise and need not be

considered as the assessment dated of 30th of May 2017 is set aside and the self assessment

would prevail.

CONCLUSION

When considering the facts and the circumstances of the case, in line with the materials

presented before this court, I am of the considered view that the Questions of law formulated

by the Appellant should be answered as set out below.

Question of law No. III – The TAC has erred in law.

This appeal is allowed.

Accordingly, the determination of the TAC dated 30th of July 2024, and the determination of

the Respondent dated 10th of June 2019 are hereby set aside and the assessment dated 30th of

May 2017 issued by the Assistant Commissioner of the Department of Inland Revenue is null

and void.
