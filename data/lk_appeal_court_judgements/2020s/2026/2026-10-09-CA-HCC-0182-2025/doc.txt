32] In Kalinga Padmatilake, alias Sergeant Elpitiya vs The Director

General, Commission to Investigate Allegations of Bribery or Corruption,

SC Appeal No. 99/2007, DECIDED ON: 30/07/2009 reported in [2009] 2 SLR

151 and [2010] BLR 67, Her Ladyship CHANDRA EKANAYAKE, J. held;

“it is the paramount duty of the Court to consider entire evidence of

a witness brought on record in the examination-in-chief, cross-examination

and re-examination. In other words, Courts must take an overall view of the

evidence of each witness” [Emphasis is added]

33] In that case it is noted what is proof of beyond reasonable doubt as;

“…proof beyond reasonable doubt does not mean proof beyond the shadow

of doubt. The benefit of doubt to which the accused is entitled, is reasonable

Page 13 of 14

doubt; the doubt which rational thinking men will reasonably,

honestly and conscientiously entertain and not the doubt of a timid

mind. Thus, test is whether rational thinking men will reasonably, honestly

and conscientiously entertain the story of the Plaintiff…” [Emphasis is

added]

34] In that case Her Ladyship further cited with approval;

“Sir John Woodroffe & Amir Ali had to say in their work on - “Law of

Evidence- 18th Edition- Vol. 1 at pg. 471: -

“No hard and fast rule can be laid down about appreciation of evidence. It

is after all a question of fact and each case has to be decided on the facts

as they stand in that particular case. Where a witness makes two

inconsistent statements in his evidence with regard to a material fact and

circumstance, the testimony of such a witness becomes unreliable and

unworthy of credence.”

“John Woodroffe & Amir Ali in their aforementioned book on Law of

Evidence - (at page 325):

“For a doubt to stand in the way of conviction of guilt, it must be a real doubt

and a reasonable doubt–a doubt which after full and fair consideration of

the evidence, the judge really, on reasonable grounds, entertains.”

35] Moreover, His Lordship Yasantha Kodagoda PC J. in Officer-in-Charge

Special Crimes Division v. Mananage Sunil Dharmapala SC Appeal No.

155/14 decided on 28/06/2021, clearly held what is reasonable doubt as;

“a ‘reasonable doubt’ means a doubt in respect of which a valid reason

can be attributed. For a ‘doubt’ to be recognised as amounting to a

‘reasonable doubt’, the ground for the development of the doubt must be

objective and reason based. There should be a logical basis for the

entertaining of the doubt. That is the distinction between a ‘mere doubt’

and a ‘reasonable doubt’.” [Emphasis is added]

36] In this case, the Accused Appellant created with regard to the medical

condition of the Victim and him. This is logical and can be treated as a

reasonable doubt. The Accused is presumed to be innocent until guilt is proved.

If the Accused creates a reasonable doubt, the Prosecution is not left with a

call evidence in rebuttal. Section says;

“If any evidence is adduced on behalf of the accused the prosecuting

counsel may with the leave of the Judge call witnesses in rebuttal”

37] In this matter, the Prosecution has failed to counter the reasonable doubt

created by the Accused Appellant. I hold that the benefit of doubt must be given

to the Accused. Overall, the evidence of the Victim is unsatisfactory. I hold that

the conviction is untenable. Thus, I set aside the conviction and sentence, the

Accused- Appellant is acquitted and discharged.
