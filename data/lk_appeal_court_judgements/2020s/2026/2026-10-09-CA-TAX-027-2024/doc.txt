The tax in dispute is against the assessment made for the taxable period 2012/2013 and the

FACTUAL BACKGROUND

The Commercial Leasing and Finance PLC (hereinafter will be referred to as the Appellant)

is a Public limited liability company incorporated and domiciled in Sri Lanka as a registered

Finance Company. The principal activities of the Appellant are leasing, hire purchase, term

loans, operating leases, vehicle hiring, loans, and other financial services.

The Appellant has submitted the return for the taxable period 2012/2013 and the Senior

Deputy Commissioner of Department of Inland Revenue (sometimes will be referred to as the

Assessor) has rejected the said return by disallowing the claim made by the Appellant in the

intimation letter dated 23rd of November 2015. Accordingly, the Assessor has issued the

Notice of Assessment dated 27th of November 2015 based on the reasons set out in the

intimation letter.

The Appellant being aggrieved by the decision of the Assessor, made an appeal to the

Commissioner General of Inland Revenue (hereinafter will be referred to as the Respondent).

The Respondent in his determination dated 20th of December 2017 determined the appeal by

reducing the assessment issued by the Assessor. The Appellant being dissatisfied with the

said determination of the Respondent, made an appeal to the Tax Appeals Commission

(TAC) on the basis that the assessment issued by the Senior Deputy Commissioner of the

Department of Inland Revenue is time barred. The TAC after considering the submissions

made by both parties made the determination dated 13th of November 2023 in favor of the

Respondent.

Being dissatisfied with the said determination, the Appellant has requested the TAC to cause

a Case Stated to the Court of Appeal on the following Questions of Law.

The Questions of law No. I, II, III, and IV are in relation to the validity of the assessment

However, the parties have agreed to consider the Question of Law No. II which is in relation

to the time limitation in issuing an assessment by the Assessor, as the preliminary issue.

Hence, this court will confine its determination on the preliminary issue raised.

Question of Law No. II

II. Has the Tax Appeal Commission erred in law in failing to determine that the

Deputy Commissioner had not made an assessment of income tax on the Appellant

for the Year of Assessment 2012/2013 in accordance with the provisions of the

Inland Revenue Act No.10 of 2006 as amended and/or within the period permitted

by the Inland Revenue Act No.10 of 2006 as amended to make an assessment on

the Appellant under the said Act?

It is the contention of the Appellant that the taxable return for the period of 2012/2013 was

made by the Appellant on 28th of October 2013 and it was rejected by the Assessor for the

reasons set out in the intimation letter dated 23rd of November 2015. Accordingly, the notice

of assessment dated 27th of November 2015 is posted by the Respondent on 10th of December

2015 which was received thereafter. Thereby it is the contention of the Appellant that the

assessment issued by the Assessor is time barred under section 163(5) of the IRA 2006.

However, it is the Respondent’s position that the said intimation letter is dated 23rd of

November 2015, which is received by the Appellant within the time period under the IRA

2006. On this basis, the Respondent argues that the time limitation mentioned under section

163(5) is only for the purpose of ‘making an assessment’ and it does not refer to the

subsequent action of notifying the tax payer by way of the Notice of Assessment.

Accordingly, the Respondent contends that the said time period under section 163(5) does not

apply to the notice of assessment dated 27th of November 2015 and therefore, the assessment

is within the time period prescribed by law. In supporting their position, the Respondent

relies on the position adopted in the cases of Commissioner General of Income Tax V.

Chettinnad Corporation Limited1 , D.M.S. Fernando V. Mohideen Ismail2 and also the

approach taken in Stafford Motor Company (Private) Limited V. Commissioner General of

1 (1954) 55 N.L.R. 553. 2 (1982) 1 Sri. L.R. 222.

Inland Revenue3 which ultimately relied on the English case of Honig and Others V.

Starsfield.4

The TAC in agreement with the arguments made by the Respondent, held in the following

manner in the determination dated 13th of November 2023.

“According to section 164 of the Act, the 'Notice of Assessment included the

amount of tax that has already been assessed. Hence, the Notice of Assessment would

be sent after making an Assessment, therefore the time bar provision which is

indicated in section 163(5) does not depend on the serving of the Notice of

Assessment. But the Notice of Assessment should be sent within a reasonable time

period or has to be "at or about the time" of an Assessment is made on.

This position was well established in the decided case of Honig & Others Vs.

Sarasfield (TC Vol 30 page 337) by Fox L.J. with following observation;

…

The Section again draws a clear distinction between the assessment and the notice of

assessment, and shows that they are different, the assessment being in no way

dependent upon the service of the notice. In the lights of the above, it is clear that the

making an Assessment is an independent act covered by section 163 of the IR Act and

the IRD has clearly complied with that. Further, serving of the notice of assessment

which is covered by section 164 of the IR Act is an activity which is independent from

making an assessment. IRD has complied with the act of serving the assessment in

terms of section 164.

As such, the Assessment had been made on time and valid in law.

Therefore, I observed that, the Appellant has pointed out the fact that, the dates of

posting and receiving the Assessment were made after the time bar of 30.11.2015 and

the time bar issue has been raised along with supportive case descriptions in favour

of the Appellants' arguments. The Respondent argued that, the time bar issue does not

arise according to sections 163 (5) and 164 of the Act, and it is in a more descriptive

manner explained along with case laws supportive in favour of his arguments.

Therefore, I agree with the counter arguments and facts concluded by the Respondent

and, revealed that the time bar issue does not arise in the present case.”

The TAC having considered the view taken up in the case of Honig, has interpreted the

section 163(5) of the IRA 2006 by making a distinction between the action of ‘making the

assessment’ and the subsequent action of ‘notice of assessment’.

The section 163(5) of the IRA 2006 clearly establishes that for a year of assessment made

prior to 1st of April 2013, the Assessor shall be required to issue an assessment for the said

taxable period on or before two years from the 30th day of November of the immediately

succeeding year of Assessment.

For ease of reference, I shall reproduce the section 163(5) of the IRA 2006 below;

“(5) Subject to the provisions of section 72, no assessment of the income tax payable

under this Act by any person or partnership –

(a) who or which has made a return of his or its income on or before the

thirtieth day of November of the year of assessment immediately succeeding

that year of assessment,

(i) where such year of assessment is any year of assessment

commencing prior to April 1, 2013, shall be made after the

expiry of a period of two years from the thirtieth day of

November of the immediately succeeding year of assessment;

and

(ii) where such year of assessment is any year of assessment

commencing on or after April 1, 2013, shall be made after the

Page 9 of 12

expiry of a period of eighteen months from the thirtieth day of

November of the immediately succeeding year of assessment:”

When considering the facts and circumstances of the case, the year of assessment for the

taxable period 2012/2013 commences prior to 1st of April 2013, therefore, the Assessor is

required by law under section 163(5) of IRA 2006, to issue an assessment within two years of

time period from the 30th day of November of the immediately succeeding year of

assessment. Hence, the Assessor is required to issue an assessment on or before 29th of

November 2015.

Before I delve into the application of law to the facts of the case, I will initially set out the

timeline of the events below;

Taxable return made by the Appellant for

the period 2012/2013

28th of October 2013

The return received by the Respondent 30th of November 2013

Time bar for the Assessment to be issued 29th of November 2015

Letter of Intimation 23rd of November 2015

Notice of Assessment dated 27th of November 2015

Notice of Assessment posted 10th of December 2015

Notice of Assessment received 11th of December 2015

When considering the above timeline, it can be observed that although the notice of

assessment was dated 27th November 2015, it was posted and received by the Appellant only

after the expiry of the time period prescribed by law. Further, the dates in relation to the

posting the Notice of Assessment (10th of December 2015) and the Date in which it was

received by the Appellant (11th of December 2015) has never been challenged.

Further, in interpreting the time limitation under section 163 of IRA 2006, it needs to be

understood that the subsequent section 164 requires every assessment made to be

communicated to the taxpayer by way of a notice of assessment.

Page 10 of 12

When considering the facts and circumstances of the present case, the tax liability referred to

in the letter of intimation differs from the amount stated in the notice of assessment, as the

notice of assessment also includes the penalty for late payment. In such circumstances, it

would be unreasonable to expect the taxpayer to exercise their rights or take further action

solely on the basis of the letter of intimation, before receiving the final computation of the tax

liability by way of a notice of assessment. Therefore, it is my view that until the final

computation of the tax liability is communicated by way of a notice of assessment, the

process of assessment remains incomplete.

This position is also considered and established in the case of Fonterra Brands Lanka (Private)

Limited V. The Commissioner General of Department of Inland Revenue and others (2025) SC

Appeal 187/2014. and this judicial pronouncement being the latest position taken up by the Supreme

Court, this court is bound by it. His Lordship Thurairaja, PC, J having considered the previous

judgments in Commissioner General of Income Tax V. Chettinnad Corporation Limited ,

D.M.S. Fernando V. Mohideen Ismail , Stafford Motor Company (Private) Limited V.

Commissioner General of Inland Revenue and also the English case Honig and Others V.

Starsfield, arrived at a ruling that the time limitation under section 163(5) applies not only to

the act of ‘making the assessment’ by the Assessor, but it applies to the communication to

the tax payer by way of notice of assessment, as such communication completes the process

of assessment.

This court having relied in the case of Fonterra Brands in many previous instances have held

that the notice of assessment being the ultimate communication given to the tax payer

regarding the tax liability, needs to be given within the time period under section 163(5) of

IRA 2006 as the said action completes the process of the assessment. Nevertheless in the

present circumstance I do not find any exceptional circumstances to deviate from the

previous approach taken by this court. Therefore, I am of the view that the TAC has erred in

holding that the assessment issued by the Assessor is within the time period.

Hence, for the foregoing reasons, I answer the Question of Law No. II in the Affirmative.
