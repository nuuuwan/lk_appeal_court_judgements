Judgement

Introduction

The counsel on all sides agreed that both Applications can be disposed by one judgement;

therefore, the judgement given in this Application applies to the connected Writ Application

bearing No. 381/2019.

The Petitioner is a body incorporated in terms of Act No. 07 of 2007, and it can sue and be

sued in its corporate name. It is a subsidiary of a large conglomerate known as the “Finlay

Group of Sri Lanka” and carries on the business of inter alia processing and producing tea and

tea bags for export.

Further, it has a subsidiary, namely, “Finlay Properties (Pvt) Limited and Finlay Teas (Pvt)

Limited”. The Finlay parent company was incorporated in 1893 and commenced its operations.

The 1st Respondent is the Minister in charge of Labour, the 2nd Respondent is the Secretary to

the Ministry, the 3rd Respondent is the Commissioner General of Labour, the 4th Respondent is

the Trade Union named the United Tea, Rubber and Local Produce Workers’ Union, and the

5th to 16th Respondents were employees of the Petitioner and members of the 4th Respondent

union.

The Petitioner, by this Application, challenges a decision of the 3rd Respondent, who took a

decision on a complaint made by the 4th Respondent, while arbitral proceedings were pending

before an arbitrator appointed under Section 4(1) of the Industrial Disputes Act No. 43 of 1950

(as amended) by the 1st Respondent.

Consequently, this matter was argued before me on 05.02.2026; hence this judgement.

The following arguments were advanced at the hearing of the case.

6

Arguments

It was argued for and on behalf of the Petitioner that making the complaint to the Deputy

Commissioner, Mr. Manoj Priyantha (the 5th respondent to the application bearing No.

381/2019), by the 4th Respondent on the basis that the Petitioner had violated Clause 25 of the

collective agreement, and entertaining such application and referring the same to the Mr.

Pathiraja, the 6th respondent to the application bearing No. 381/2019 (who is not a party to this

Application), and finally the decision taken by the 6th respondent to the said application and

later affirming the same decision of the 3rd Respondent to this Application, whose decision is

reflected in A22 is to negate or render the arbitration proceedings and the decision therein to

be taken by the Arbitrator to render the same as negatory. Therefore, it is argued on behalf of

the Petitioner that by confirming the 3rd respondent of the said decision contained in A22 by

the decision contained in A29 annexed to the present Petition, is capricious, irrational, and

illegal.

However, it is argued for and on behalf of the Respondents that in view of Section 10A of the

Industrial Disputes Act, No. 43 of 1950 (as amended), any party to a collective agreement can

obtain an interpretation from the Commissioner General of Labour or any officer thereof on

any clause of such increment. Therefore, the complaint made by the 4th Respondent to Mr.

Pathiraja (the 6th respondent to the application bearing No. 381/2019) is rational, and after

inquiry, the decision taken by Mr. Pathiraja, whose decision is annexed to this Application as

A22, is justified.

It is further argued on behalf of the Respondents that the affirmation by the 3rd respondent of

the said decision taken by Mr. Pathiraja (the 6th respondent to the application bearing No.

381/2019), is rational and within the law.

7

In reply, it is further argued for and on behalf of the Petitioner that the embarking upon an

inquiry to give an interpretation to Clause 25 when there is already an ongoing arbitration

process on the referral made by the 1st Respondent, the Minister, is not rationalised.

Further, it is argued in this present Application for and on behalf of the Petitioner that the

affirmation of the decision by the 3rd respondent of the said decision taken by Mr. Pathiraja

reflected in A22 is for improper motives.

In addition to that, it is argued for and on behalf of the Petitioner that the Petitioner entertained

a legitimate expectation that the 3rd Respondent would re-consider the decision of Mr. Pathiraja

contained in A22 because pursuant to the settlement entered into between the parties in the

application bearing No. 381/2019, the 3rd Respondent had undertaken to reconsider his decision

contained in X50, a document annexed to the said application bearing No. 381/2019. Therefore,

without considering the same, and giving an interpretation which is acceptable and reasonable,

other than the decision given by Mr. Pathiraja, cannot be justified.

The Petitioner’s position

The Petitioner asserts that the 5th to 16th Respondents are employees of the Petitioner and

members of the 4th Respondent Trade Union. The Petitioner and the 4th Respondent had entered

into a collective agreement, which is marked as X37 to the application bearing No. 381/2019

and the 4th Respondent had entered into the said collective agreement for and on behalf of the

employees of the Petitioner.

The Petitioner is mainly engaged in the business of processing tea and tea bags for export

purposes, and the Petitioner claims that it has about 14,000 employees employed under it.

The Petitioner alleges that in 2017, the 5th to 16th Respondents had started trade union action

known as a ‘go-slow’ campaign, at the instigation of the 4th Respondent, thus causing

8

disruption to the operations of the Petitioner. This included the failure to perform the following

functions in the production process;

1. Carton forming in carton machines;

2. Packing in overwrapping machines; and

3. Value-added packs, such as inserting tea bags into inner cartons.

Consequently, the Petitioner requested them to give up their trade union action, but they did

not pay any heed to the said request. Therefore, the Petitioner asserts that it was compelled to

give them a warning by letter dated 21.06.2017, which is marked as A3 annexed to the Petition.

Despite that, they continued with the trade union actions, thus resulting in further disruption of

the production process of the Petitioner. Therefore, the Petitioner asserts that it was compelled

to issue show cause letters to the 5th to 16th Respondents, which the Petitioner has marked as

A4 annexed to the Petition.

Thereafter, the said Respondents responded to the show cause letters issued to them.

Accordingly, they submitted their letters of explanation dated 07.07.2017, 10.07.2017, and

11.07.2017, respectively, and the same letters are marked as A5(1) to A5(13), respectively.

According to the Petitioner, in the said letters, they had taken the position to justify their trade

union action. Thus, the Petitioner asserts that they had taken the stand that they had no other

option but to continue with the trade union action. Consequently, the Petitioner was compelled

to take disciplinary action against them.

Accordingly, by letter dated 20.07.2017, the Petitioner appointed an inquirer and informed the

5th to 16th Respondents that an inquirer has been appointed to hold a domestic inquiry since

their explanations were not satisfactory.

The inquiry commenced on 17.08.2017, and after the inquiry, the 5th to 16th Respondents were

found guilty by the inquirer, of all the charges levelled against them; this included

9

insubordination to the management, disruption of production by engaging in illegal trade union

actions which resulted in disrupting the production processes of the Petitioner, and thus,

causing heavy loss and damage to the Petitioner.

Therefore, thereafter, the management of the Petitioner decided to terminate their employment,

and accordingly, they were informed by letters of termination marked as A10(1) to A10(12),

respectively, annexed to the Petition.

Thereafter, the 4th Respondent responded to the said letters of termination and insisted that the

said 5th to 16th Respondents should be immediately reinstated; otherwise, they would take trade

union action against the Petitioner, including calling for a strike.

However, the Petitioner asserts that in the meantime, certain employees walked out on the

following day of the termination of the employment of the 5th to 16th Respondents, at the

instigation of the 4th Respondent.

In the meantime, the 4th Respondent made a complaint to the Commissioner General, the 3rd

Respondent, about the termination of the 5th to 16th Respondents; and consequently, the matter

was referred to the 1st Respondent as an industrial dispute.

Accordingly, the 1st Respondent referred the matter to arbitration in terms of Section 4(1) of

the Industrial Disputes Act. However, after preliminary objections were raised by the Petitioner,

the first arbitration was cancelled, and subsequently, another arbitrator was appointed in terms

of Arbitration bearing No. 3 of 2018. After pleadings were completed, the arbitration proceeded.

The Writ Application bearing No. 381/2019

However, the 4th Respondent made another complaint to Mr. Manoj Priyantha, an Assistant

Commissioner of Labour, who was cited as the 5th Respondent in the case bearing No. 381/2019,

with regard to the violation of the Collective Agreement which had been entered into between

10

the 4th Respondent and the Petitioner, particularly Clause 25 of the said Agreement. The said

complaint is dated 04.12.2017 and was marked as X38 in the said case bearing No. 381/2019.

Thereby, Mr. Manoj Priyantha had referred it to Mr. Pathiraja, a Deputy Commissioner.

However, after notice, the Petitioner received notice with regard to the same by X39. The said

notice had been sent by the Deputy Commissioner General of Labour, Mr. Pathiraja, requiring

the Petitioner to appear for the inquiry. At the inquiry, the Petitioner raised preliminary

objections before the inquiring officer, Mr. Pathiraja, who was cited as the 6th Respondent, to

the said application bearing No. 381/2019, which is reflected in the proceedings of the case

bearing No. 381/2019.

However, after several dates of inquiry, correspondence, letters, and submissions made by the

parties, the Deputy Commissioner, Mr. Pathiraja (the 6th Respondent to the said application

bearing No. 381/2019), took a decision reflected in X46, a document marked in the case bearing

No. 381/2019, that the Petitioner had violated Clause 25 of the collective agreement, since the

Petitioner had not followed the provision to issue a show cause letter within 30 days after

finding the employees guilty of the charges in the domestic inquiry.

However, since no action was taken by the Petitioner pursuant to X46 of the said case, the 5th

Respondent to the Application bearing No. 381/2019, in the case filed action in the Magistrate’s

Court, as reflected in X52 and X53. The said action was also taken after discussions between

the parties before the Commissioner, the 3rd Respondent.

The proceedings of the said decision are also marked in the said case as X50.

However, upon a perusal of the said case record bearing No. 381/2019, I found that the said

application bearing No. 381/2019, had been resolved between the parties by entering into

mutually agreed terms of settlement dated 24.01.2020, which is marked as A26 to the instant

11

Application. The parties, as well as their representatives and their respective Attorneys-at-Law,

had signed the terms of settlement and filed the same of record.

Accordingly, the said application had been withdrawn.

The Petitioner’s present grievance

However, the Petitioner asserts in the Petition in the instant Application that pursuant to the

terms of the settlement entered into between the parties on 24.01.2020, since the Petitioner

withdrew the said application bearing No. 381/2019, the parties agreed to reconsider the

documents marked as A22 annexed to this Petition, and X46 annexed to the case bearing No.

381/2019, which is the same document wherein a decision was taken by the 6th Respondent to

the said application bearing No. 381/2019.

However, he is not a respondent to this Application, but a subordinate officer of the 3rd

Respondent, and it is pursuant to that decision that the said application had been instituted.

However, it is alleged that according to the terms of settlement in the said application bearing

No. 381/2019, there was no proper reconsideration of the decision of Mr. Pathiraja marked as

A22. After the settlement in application bearing No. 381/2019, there was a delay of a meeting

due to various circumstances. Finally, a meeting was held between the Petitioner and the 3rd

Respondent; therefore, the Petitioner alleges that the 3rd Respondent agreed to reconsider the

decision taken by Mr. Pathiraja contained in A22, which he had failed to do.

Nevertheless, the Petitioner received a communication marked as A29 in this Application,

indicating that reconsideration of A22 was not necessary.

Despite that fact, the Petitioner asserts that, at the meeting held on 10.11.2022, the 3rd

Respondent indicated that there is possibility of giving an alternative interpretation to Clause

12

25(5), other than the interpretation given by the Deputy Commissioner General, Mr. Pathiraja,

as reflected in A22.

Therefore, the Petitioner challenges the said A29 and A22 in this Application, on the basis that

A22 is ultra vires and A29 is unreasonable, capricious, and without any evidence.

Objections

The 1st to 3rd Respondents have filed their Objections. Although the 4th Respondent had been

represented at the stage of support for notice, there is no representation thereafter, and therefore,

I cannot find any Statement of Objections from the 4th Respondent.

According to the 1st to 3rd Respondents, it is their position that the 3rd Respondent or his

subordinate officers had taken the decision independently of the arbitration proceedings or the

decision pending in terms of Section 10A of the Industrial Disputes Act before the arbitrator,

and independently of the proceedings or referral thereto, on a complaint made by the 4th

Respondent for and on behalf of the 5th to 12th Respondents, in terms of the interpretation of

the collective agreement, which the 3rd Respondent is empowered to do.

Therefore, the final decision pursuant to A22 taken by the Respondent is justified. As such, the

1st to 3rd Respondents have moved to dismiss this application.

This was argued before me. However, the parties were directed to file written submissions.

Nevertheless, none of the Respondents have filed written submissions; only the Petitioner has

filed written submissions.

Issues to be considered

In view of the pleadings by both parties by way of the Petition and Statement of Objections,

respectively, and the arguments advanced orally and by way of Written Submissions, the

following questions arise for my consideration;

13

(i) Whether there was an industrial dispute between the Petitioner and the 4th Respondent,

who made a complaint for and on behalf of the 5th to 16th Respondents;

(ii) Whether such dispute was referred to arbitration by the 1st Respondent;

(iii) Whether it is reasonable for the 3rd Respondent, or any other officer of the Department

of the 3rd Respondent, to entertain any complaint made by the 4th Respondent against

the Petitioner arising out of the same dispute, when the said arbitration is pending;

(iv) Whether Mr. Pathiraja has given an interpretation to Clause 25 in his decision marked

as A22;

(v) If the answer to the above is in the negative, whether the decision and affirmation made

by the 3rd Respondent by A29 of the decision taken by Mr. Pathiraja contained in A22,

is rationalised and justified

Now I will consider the matters for clarity in chronological order.

Industrial dispute

The Petitioner complaints that there had been a trade union action taken ex parte by the 5th to

16th Respondents as the members of the 4th Respondent, who is the Trade Union, against the

Petitioner within the course of their employment. The Petitioner asserts that the said trade union

action resulted in the disruption of the smooth functioning of the Petitioner’s production; thus,

resulted in loss and damages to the Petitioner. Therefore, the Petitioner asserts that the 5th to

16th Respondents were initially given a warning, and thereafter, was given a warning in writing.

Despite all that, the 5th to 16th Respondents had continued with their trade union action by going

slow and cause disruption in the production, and thereby, affecting the output and sales, which

resulted in the loss of profit and damage to the Petitioner. As such, the Petitioner asserts that

the Petitioner was compelled to call for explanations, which was followed by letters calling for

explanations and justification of their actions by the 5th to 16th Respondents. Therefore, the

14

Petitioner asserts that the Petitioner had to take disciplinary actions against the 5th to 16th

Respondents.

Consequently, an Inquirer had been appointed, and after giving proper notice, there was a

domestic inquiry on certain charges elaborated in this case and morefully in case bearing No.

381/2019, based on insubordination, causing disruption, and causing loss and damages to the

Petitioner.

After the said disciplinary inquiry, the 5th to 16th Respondents were found guilty, and the report

had been forwarded to the Petitioner, who received it on 04.09.2017.

Thereafter, the Petitioner has sent the letter of termination with show cause, and thus, the 5th to

16th Respondents’ services were terminated, and thereupon an industrial dispute has arisen

between the Petitioner and the 4th Respondent, who acted for and on behalf of the 5th to 16th

Respondents. Accordingly, on information made to the Minister, the 1st Respondent, has caused

to appoint an arbitrator in terms of Section 4(1) of the Industrial Disputes Act; consequently,

an arbitrator has been appointed.

Dispute referred to Arbitration

According to the referral to the Arbitrator which is filed as X17 in the application bearing No.

381/2019, which reads thus;

“The matter in dispute between the aforesaid parties is

Whether the below mentioned 18 employees who worked at Finlays Colombo

Limited had been caused injustice by being terminated from employment on

disciplinary grounds and if so, to what reliefs they are entitled”

15

According to this, the dispute has been properly referred to arbitration, the Arbitrator has taken

over the dispute, and the proceedings has properly commenced before him after appearance

was made by both parties.

Parallel proceedings by Mr. Pathiraja

However, pending the said arbitration proceedings and the decision thereon, the 4th Respondent,

by his complaint dated 04.12.2017 (marked as X38 in application bearing No. 381/2019), made

to the 5th respondent to the said application bearing No. 381/2019, Mr. Manoj Priyantha,

alleged that the Petitioner had violated Clause 25 of the collective agreement, in which the

Petitioner had not given due notice to the Respondents in the process of terminating their

employment. Therefore, the 5th respondent had referred the matter to the 6th respondent, Mr.

Pathiraja, to take a decision after inquiry. Subsequently, Mr. Pathiraja had given notice to the

Petitioner and the other Respondents to appear before him. However, the Petitioner had raised

an objection as to the maintainability of such a complaint.

Nevertheless, Mr. Pathiraja, having considered the objections and written submissions filed by

the parties, made his decision contained in A22, which is also marked as X46 in the application

bearing No. 381/2019.

However, it is the complaint of the Petitioner that firstly, the said decision taken by Mr.

Pathiraja is to render the arbitration proceedings as negatory, and secondly, there is no

interpretation given in the said decision of Mr. Pathiraja contained in A22.

Now I will consider whether Mr. Pathiraja, the 6th respondent to the application bearing No.

381/2019, has given an interpretation in his decision contained in A22 (X46 in application

bearing No. 381/2019).

For further clarity, the relevant Clause 25 of the collective agreement reads as thus;

16

25. DISCIPLINARY ACTION

Where an Employer proposes to proceed against an Employee, then- ....

5) after holding such inquiry, the Employer shall notify the Employee of the findings on

each of the charges in the show-cause notice and the punishment, if any, imposed by

the Employer. Provided that if any Employer fails to make an order except for reasons

beyond the control of the Employer on the charges in the show-cause notice within

thirty (30) working days from the conclusion of the inquiry into such charges, the

Employee shall not be able to be punished thereafter in respect of such charges and no

inference adverse to the Employee in respect of such charges shall be drawn from such

charges.

On a perusal of the decision taken by the 6th respondent, he has not given an interpretation in

such a sense, which for the purpose of clarity, I will reproduce below;

සාමූහික ගිවිසුම උල්ලංඝනය කරමින් සසේවකයින්සේ සසේවය අවසන් කිරීම පිළිබඳව එක්සත්

සත් රබර් සහ සමරට ද්‍රවය කම්කරු සමිතිය විසින් ලින්ක්සලසේ කලම්ු ලිමිටඩ් ආයතනයට

එසරහිව කරන ලද පැමිණිල්ල

උක්ත කරුණ සම්බන්ධව දෙපාර්තදම්න්ුව විසින් ඉදිරිපත් කරන ලෙ කරුණු ෙැක්ීම අනුව

ආයතනදේ විනය පරීක්ෂණ ක්‍රියාවලිය දෙපාර්තදම්න්ුව අතර ඇති සාමූහික ගිවිසුම මගින්

පාලනය වන බව තහවුරු විය. ඒ අනුව, සාමූහික ගිවිසුදම් 25.5 වගන්තිය අනුව විනය

කඩකිරීමක් සම්බන්ධදයන් පරීක්ෂණයක් පැවැත්ීම සහ එක් එක් ද ෝෙනාව සම්බන්ධදයන්

පරීක්ෂණදයන් පසුව දසායා ගන්නා කරුණු අනුව දසේවාදයෝජකයා විසින් ෙඬුවම් ලබා

දෙන්දන් නම් ඒ සඳහා දහේු ෙැක්ීදම් ලිපියක් ලබා දිය යුු බවත්, එම දහේු ෙැක්ීදම් ලිපිය,

ද ෝෙනා සම්බන්ධදයන් පරීක්ෂණ අවසන් ී වැඩ කරන දින 30 ක් ඇුළත ලබා දිය යුු බවත්,

එදසේ කිරීමට අදපාදහාසත් වන්දන් නම් එම ද ෝෙනා සම්බන්ධදයන් දසේවකයින් ෙඬුවමකට

02.එදසේ වුවත් උක්ත කරුණ සම්බන්ධදයන් ෙඬුවම් ලබා දීමට අොළ දහේු ෙැක්ීදම් ලිපිය

නියමිත කාලය ුළ ලබා දී දනාමැත. එදසේම එම ප්‍රමාෙයට දහේුව තමන්ට පාලනය කරගත

දනාහැකි දහේුවක් බව දසේවාදයෝජක විසින් තහවුරු කර දනාමැත. එබැවින් දමම කරුණ

සම්බන්ධදයන් දසේවාදයෝජක පාර්ශවය කාර්මික ආරවුල් පනදත් 40.1(අ) යටදත් වරෙක් සිදු

කර ඇති බව තහවුරු දේ. එමනිසා දමම ලිපිය ලැබී දින 14 ක් ඇුළත සාමූහික ගිවිසුදම්

ප්‍රතිපාෙන අනුව කටයුු කරන දලසත් එදසේ දනාවුනදහාත් ඉදිරි නීතිමය ක්‍රියාමාර්ග ගැනීමට

සිදුවන බවත් කාරුණිකව ෙන්වා සිටිමි.

The 6th Respondent has decided that the termination of the employment of the said employees

was a violation of the collective agreement. The Petitioner had also violated Section 4(1)(a) of

the Industrial Disputes Act. Therefore, in the event that the Petitioner failed to take the

necessary action to rectify or make restitution, otherwise to take the necessary legal action.

Therefore, it is my view that Mr. Pathiraja has not given an interpretation on whether the

Petitioner has violated Clause 25 of the collective agreement in that sense of not giving proper

notice of termination of service. Thus, affirmation of the said decision by the 3rd Respondent

as the Commissioner General of Labour, without considering whether Mr. Pathiraja had not

given proper interpretation to Clause 25 of the collective agreement and quoting Section 4(1)(a)

of the Industrial Disputes Act that the Petitioner has violated the same, and therefore, can be

charged if the Petitioner has not taken steps as directed by Mr. Pathiraja when given an

interpretation to Clause 25 of the collective agreement is also capricious, irrational, and

unjustifiable.

Further, on consideration of the entire gamut of applications made by the 4th Respondent and

the sequence of events that had taken place, it is my view that the 4th Respondent had taken

18

steps after the dispute was referred to arbitration and the arbitration proceedings had

commenced, making a complaint against the Petitioner to the subordinate officer of the 3rd

Respondent is to render the entire arbitration proceedings negatory because the Arbitrator,

according to the reference as referred to above, can go into all aspects of the dispute that had

taken place between the parties.

Therefore, without awaiting the decision of the Arbitrator, making an application in terms of

Section 10A of the Industrial Disputes Act to decide independently of the arbitration by the 3rd

Respondent or a subordinate officer of the 3rd Respondent, is not justified as the entire dispute

is already under consideration. In any case, Mr. Pathiraja has failed to give a proper

interpretation, and in fact, as reflected in the proceedings that had taken place after the

settlement of the application bearing No. 381/2019, the terms of settlement so entered in A26

are also reproduced for clarity;

Writs lie

Thereafter, as asserted by the Petitioner, the meeting had taken place between the Petitioner

and the 3rd Respondent on 10.11.2022, where the 3rd Respondent had admitted that there can

be another or alternative interpretation given as suggested by the Petitioner, which the

Petitioner legitimately expected be given by the 3rd Respondent on the interpretation of Clause

25 of the collective agreement; the 3rd Respondent has failed to do so, instead, a mere rubber

stamp affirming the decision taken by his subordinate officer Mr. Pathiraja contained in A22.

Therefore, this is prime example of just becoming a rubber stamp of a subordinate officer by a

superior officer without taking a proper stand or decision on the matter.

20

Furthermore, legitimate expectation also includes that a party is entitled to be given a reasoned

order or decision, and where such a reasoned decision is not given, it amounts to a violation of

the rules of natural justice; vide His Lordship Justice Mark Fernando judgement in the case of

Karunadasa v. Unique Gem Stores Limited and Others.1

As such, Writ of Certiorari lies to quash the decision of the 3rd Respondent contained in A29

and the decision taken by Mr. Pathiraja contained in A22 as there is no proper interpretation

given; even if there was a proper interpretation given, the whole dispute can be gone into by

the Arbitrator because it had already been referred to arbitration, which the referral of the

Arbitrator covers the entire dispute.

Conclusion

For the reasons adumbrated above, I grant relief as prayed for in prayers (c), (d), and (e) of the

Amended Petition dated 22.05.2023.
