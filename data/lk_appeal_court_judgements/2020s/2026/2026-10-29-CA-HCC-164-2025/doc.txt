JUDGMENT Amal Ranaraja. J,

1. The accused-appellant (hereinafter referred to as the ‘appellant’) has

been indicted in the High Court of Rathnapura in Case No. HCR 102-

2016. The charge in the indictment is as follows;

01. On or around the 03rd of October, 2011, in Ensaldola, within

the jurisdiction of this Court, you did stab one Hapanpellilage

Karunawathie with a knife, with such intention or knowledge,

and under such circumstances that had you by such an act

caused the death of said Hapanpellilage Karunawathie, you

would have been guilty of murder, you have committed an

offence punishable under Section 300 of the Penal Code.

2. At the conclusion of the trial, the learned High Court Judge has

convicted the appellant of the charge and sentenced him as follows;

i. To three years rigorous imprisonment and a fine of Rs. 15,000.00

with a term of 3 months simple imprisonment in default.

3

The appellant has also been directed to pay a sum of Rs.

100,000.00 to PW02 as compensation with a term of 9 months is

in default.

commenced. During this investigation PW02 has been examined by Dr.

I.L. Rathnayake, Additional Judicial Medical Officer of Colombo. The

Vide the Medico-Legal Report marked පැ02

7

The nature and site of the injury caused by the appellant, have been

corroborated by Dr. Rathnayake, who also has opined that the injury

endangered the life of PW02.

8. At the conclusion of the evidence lead by both parties the learned High

Court Judge has examined the case in a holistic manner, rather than

analysing the prosecution’s version and the appellant’s version in a

watertight compartment. He has tested each item of evidence against

the overall factual matrix and has applied the same to the ingredients

of the offence prescribed in the charge. Thereafter, the learned High

Court Judge has concluded that the prosecution successfully

discharged the burden of proof that falls upon them and rejected the

appellant’s dock statement. The learned High Court Judge has provided

comprehensive and well-founded reasoning for this determination.

Such approach accords with settled judicial principles.

9. In the case of James Silva Vs. The Republic of Sri Lanka (1980) 2 SLR

167, Rodrigo J, has stated,

“A satisfactory way to arrive at a verdict of guilt or innocence is to

consider all the matters before the Court adduced whether by the

prosecution or by the defence in its totality without

compartmentalising and, ask himself whether as a prudent man,

in the circumstances of the particular case, he believes the accused

guilty of the charge or not guilty.”

10. By refusing to compartmentalize the evidence and instead adopting a

holistic approach, the High Court has applied the correct methodology

and arrived at findings that are both cogent and sustainable. Therefore,

the disputed judgement is not perverse.

8

11. In those circumstances I am not inclined to interfere with the

conviction, disputed judgement together with the sentencing order and

proceed to affirm the same.

12. I dismiss the appeal but make no order regarding costs.

Appeal dismissed.
